EXTRA TRAVEL (accepted 7 Nov 2026 at 47e9b6b)
The documentary may quote docs/extra-travel-study.md §5. Each of these lines holds only with both labels: "synthetic demand, uncalibrated: scenario comparison, not a forecast" and "not the official ranking."
- Air market: In 2025 Jeddah–Riyadh was fifth in OAG's top ten busiest air routes by scheduled seats, about 9.8 million seats in both directions, and the fastest-growing route in that top ten (+13 % on 2024, +22 % on 2019). Accessed 7 Nov 2026. Seats are capacity, not passengers. Source: S-xt-001, S-xt-002. It is "fifth placed" in that top ten, not "fifth busiest domestic route worldwide" and not "second busiest."
- The model's pair: In the model, in 2050, rail takes about 0.42 million of 4.66 million one-way trips a year between the Riyadh and Jeddah metro areas; air keeps about 3.47 million. Source: Table 2a.
- Mode shift versus new travel: In the model, the committed lines move trips between modes and destinations; the national number of trips is the same with and without them. Source: §2.
- Extra travel: If cheaper or faster travel also creates trips (an uncalibrated sensitivity, elasticity −0.15 to −0.70), the model adds about 0.2–1.1 million trips a year nationally in 2050, 0.06–0.29 % of all trips. If they follow today's mode shares, most go by car and the appraisal gets slightly worse; if all go by rail, it gets slightly better. Either way, the committed programme's NPV changes by at most about 2.5 % at P50. Source: Table 3a. The −0.70 elasticity is a stress test, not a TAG-recommended Saudi figure.
- Land entry: In the model, Umrah visitors entering by land gain a rail path to Makkah from two Gulf crossings and one Jordan crossing; on the frozen rail share that is about 22,000 rail trips a year, and up to about 0.5 million under generous, labelled what-ifs. The busiest Jordan crossing in the data, Halat Ammar, has no rail path. Source: Table 4a. Do not fold visitor fares into the official CBA. The SAR 11.7 million to 368.6 million "if counted" fare memo changes both volume assumptions and the fare rate, not the fare alone; do not present it as a constant-fare range.
- Hajj: Hajj numbers are set by permits: a railway can change how pilgrims travel, not how many come. Every scenario holds Hajj fixed. Source: §4.
- Japan caution: Japan has an extensive high-speed rail network covering the country, yet the world's second and third busiest air routes in 2025 were Japanese domestic routes (OAG). Source: S-xt-007. This is a country-level caution, not proof that both named Japanese air pairs have equivalent high-speed-rail alternatives.

Do not write a pilgrim or visitor vignette around these lines. Land-entry paths are frozen v1-r5 graph results; do not narrate them as independently surveyed real-world connectivity.
