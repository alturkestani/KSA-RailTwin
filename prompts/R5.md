## Tasks
1. **Standards and parameters**: `docs/cba.md` opens with the standards used (HMT Green Book, DfT TAG incl. A2.4, EU CBA Guide 2014, World Bank guidance, any Saudi guidance or "none found, <date>") and a departures table. Parameters in `cities/ksa/assumptions/cba_*.yaml`: KSA value of time by purpose (assumption with a sourced range and the derivation, e.g. from wages/GDP per capita; labelled), reliability values, crash costs, carbon values (sourced schedules), emission factors by mode, discount rates (social, commercial/financial, declining long-term per the Green Book), horizons 30/40/60 with residual value method. Every row registered.
2. **Benefit streams** (`economics/benefits.py`), each as a function of R3/R4 outputs with the channel it uses and the ones it excludes: travel-time savings by purpose (rule of a half), reliability, safety (road crashes avoided from VKT shift), emissions and air quality, decongestion (Jeddah from the R4 TrafficTwin matched-control runs; elsewhere VKT-based; never both for one trip), freight shift road→rail, agglomeration and wider economic impacts (TAG A2.4 method; reported separately), land value uplift as capture potential only (not added to benefits), tourism and Hajj/Umrah capacity (only where sourced), regional development and Vision 2030 alignment as a qualitative scorecard (`docs/cba-scorecard.md`, not monetised unless sourced). The no-double-counting matrix is written in `docs/cba.md` and checked by a test.
3. **Cases**: `cities/ksa/cba/<candidate>.yaml` for every S2–S5 candidate (and S1 vs S0 as the committed-programme check), each naming its matched baseline, scenario, population scenario set, cost run id, demand run id, horizons and rates. `uv run python -m economics.cba run --case ... --seed 1 --out runs/cba/<id>/` writes `results.json` (NPV, BCR with and without WEIs, EIRR, FIRR, payback, fiscal cost to the state, all P10/P50/P90 per horizon and discount rate), `draws.parquet`, `tornado.csv/.png`, `switching_values.json` ("demand must be X % of forecast for BCR = 1"; capex, VoT and discount-rate switching values), `meta.json`.
4. **Monte Carlo** (`montecarlo.py`): joint draws over capex and schedule (R1c uplifts), ridership shortfall, VoT, discount rate, opex, construction duration; 10,000 draws by default, seeded; correlation assumptions registered. Tornado from one-at-a-time swings. Shared draws with R1c's `costs.rcf` where both apply (same seed → same multipliers; test it).
5. **Distributional analysis** (`distribution.py`, method from R1d `docs/social.md`): benefits and costs by region and income band; who gains, who pays (fiscal incidence), who is left out (towns without a station, freight-only corridors); `docs/distribution.md` with tables per candidate, labelled and sourced.
6. **Summary**: `runs/cba/summary.parquet` (one row per candidate × population scenario × horizon × rate with every output as P10/P50/P90) and the `docs/cba.md` results section rendered by `uv run python -m economics.cba report` (never hand-typed): per family, the candidates ranked by P50 BCR with their P10–P90 band, switching values, and the explicit statement that these are scenario comparisons on synthetic, uncalibrated demand.
7. **Tests** on the fixture CBA case: discounting identities (NPV of a known stream), declining-rate schedule, no-double-counting check (a benefit counted twice fails), determinism of draws, switching-value solver converges.
8. **Handoff** (`milestone-handoff`): `make test`, `red-team-verifier` (double counting, standards departures, ranges, sources), commits per task, status block in `docs/status/r5.md` (cases run, P50 BCR range across families with run ids, draws per case, disk used). End with `R5: done` or `R5: blocked - <reason>`.
