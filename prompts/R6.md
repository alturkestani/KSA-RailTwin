# R6 — Network topology optimisation (autoresearch loop + classical search)

## Goal
Find the strongest national rail network layouts for 2050, robust across the low/medium/high population cases, and deliver a short Pareto set of layouts (not one "best" map) with full audit trails.

## Preconditions (hard gate; check before any search run)
1. R4 merged: rail graph with transfers, running-time model within ±15% of the HHR timetable (amended from ±10% by Tariq at CP6, 3 Nov 2026, matching H1a: the R5 gate figure is −12.2 % against the 28–30 min secondary midpoint, no official dated timetable found), Landbridge/HHR artefact fixed, air and bus recalibrated.
2. R5 merged: CBA/appraisal (Green Book rates, P10/P50/P90 corridor costs, Monte Carlo).
3. Judge back-test passes: S0 and S1 reproduce their R4/R5 results within 1%.
If any precondition fails, stop and report; do not search.

## Design
### A. The editable surface (the only thing the search may change)
- One file: scenarios/candidate.yaml. Fields: corridors included (from the 76 in S2–S5 plus any agent-proposed links on the existing network graph), station locations (snapped to the zone and settlement register), design speed class, single or double track, electrification, and phasing (2030/2035/2040/2050).
- Agent-proposed new links must be built from network/ geometry and Copernicus DEM terrain costing; no free-hand lines.

### B. The judge (locked, read-only)
- judge/ is a frozen copy of the demand model + appraisal at the R5 tag, run from a read-only worktree. Its hash is recorded at R6 start and checked on every evaluation; a mismatch aborts the run.
- The search agent may not edit judge/, the data, calibration constants, the cost tables or the scoring code. The verifier enforces this with a diff check on every kept commit.
- Output per layout: NPV and BCR (P50 plus P10/P90), capex, rail trips and passenger-km, ridership per route-km, accessibility gain (population within 60/120 minutes of Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah, Madinah and Dammam), CO2 saved, and the HHR and Landbridge loads.
- Score: robust NPV = the minimum of NPV across the low/medium/high population cases at P50 cost. Report the others alongside.
- Hard constraints (an infeasible layout is rejected, not penalised): a capex ceiling, with sweeps at 3 budget levels you propose with justification; Makkah and Madinah served; gradient and terrain limits; no corridor through protected areas without flagging; and each phase deliverable on its own.

### C. Search, as two complementary engines
1. Classical baseline: a genetic algorithm (or MILP where it fits) over the corridor include/exclude and phasing space, many seeds. This gives the reference Pareto front (robust NPV vs capex vs accessibility).
2. Autoresearch loop (Karpathy-style): the agent proposes one edit to candidate.yaml with a one-line hypothesis; the judge scores it; keep and commit if robust NPV improves (or it adds a new non-dominated point), else revert. Log every attempt to runs/r6/results.tsv (commit, hypothesis, scores, kept or reverted, cost). The agent also seeds the GA with its best ideas, and the GA's front seeds the agent.
- Fast mode: a reduced-fidelity judge (fewer zones or a cached demand matrix) for screening. Every layout that is kept or on the front is re-scored at full fidelity before it counts. Report the fast-vs-full rank correlation.

### D. Guardrails
- Existing runaway guard and cost log apply. R6 phase estimate: propose one with justification in the first status line; per-evaluation cost logged.
- No blind retries; stop and report after 20 consecutive non-improving agent edits, or if the front hasn't moved in 2 GA restarts.
- Overfitting checks: hold out the high-population case and the P90 costs from the search score in one run, then test on them; flag any layout whose rank drops by more than 5 places.
- Red-team verifier audits the final set: judge hash intact, no judge/data edits, results reproducible from the commit and seed.

## Deliverables
- runs/r6/: results.tsv, the Pareto front (CSV + plot), the top 5 layouts as scenario YAMLs, each with maps (existing + proposed lines, stations, phasing), a scorecard and the reasoning for why it wins.
- Comparison of the top 5 against S1–S5 and against the GA-only and agent-only bests (which engine found what).
- docs/r6-topology.md: method, assumptions (A-* register), sensitivities, limits ("scenario comparison on a modelled demand base, not a forecast").
- CP6 report with decisions for me: which layouts go to R7 delivery modelling.
