# Saudi administrative regions and governorates (working list for W2 coverage)

Access date for every source: 2026-10-30 (Asia/Riyadh). Source ids refer to `docs/sources/index.csv`; conflicts are in `docs/sources/conflicts.md` (C-010, C-011).

## Status in one paragraph

- **Regions: 13.** Verified on GASTAT's own site (S-070), though the locator is weak because the phrase sits in a 'Read Also' item.
- **Governorate count as published by GASTAT: not found within the hour.** The GASTAT census portal (portal.saudicensus.sa) did not resolve on 2026-10-30 (S-072, S-090). The Ministry of Interior regions page lists no governorates. The Law of Provinces text on boe.gov.sa was not reached.
- **Secondary totals disagree:** 150 (S-073, S-076), 139 (S-074), 136 (S-075), and 135, 134 and 118 in search snippets.
- **Working universe:** the list below follows the 150 governorate rows of the census-2022 administrative list republished by citypopulation.de, which credits GASTAT (S-073). That list counts each region's capital city as a governorate row. It is the most complete list found, so using it avoids silent omissions (rule 2).
- **Two flagged extra rows** come from the Arabic Wikipedia list (Abanat in Al-Qassim, Al-Bayda in the Eastern Province), giving 152 rows. They are marked `conflict` and must be confirmed or dropped against the primary list.
- **Status:** every row is `secondary` until GASTAT's own table is read. `docs/coverage.md` reconciles to the primary count once found; until then it reconciles to 150 and notes the conflict.

**Arabic names.** Governorate Arabic names were matched from the Arabic Wikipedia list by transliteration (secondary). Region Arabic names come from the ISO 3166-2:SA table on Wikipedia (secondary). A native-speaker check against GASTAT is pending. Al-Amwah (Asir) is not in the Arabic list; its Arabic name is unverified.
