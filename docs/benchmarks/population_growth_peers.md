# Population-growth peers: how growing and shrinking populations planned rail and housing around stations

Scope: W5 task 4 (CP1 candidates). Question: how countries with growing populations and good rail, and countries with shrinking populations, planned rail networks and housing around them: transit-oriented development (TOD), new towns, station-area policy, rail-plus-property and land value capture. Converted values: see README.md. Money is shown nominal, in the currency and price year the source prints. Evidence is mostly qualitative: policy texts (grade A) describe intent and rules; outcome evidence is one city-produced case (Toyama), one regression abstract (Korea), one federal self-reported status report (Germany) and an operator's accounts (MTR). No outcome study was found that isolates a rail station's effect on a town's housing or population with a counterfactual that the sources themselves report.

"Growing" and "shrinking" refer to the national population series from the World Bank (WDI). National totals can hide regional decline (Toyama, eastern Germany, the regions of Korea), so regional evidence is reported separately.

### System facts per case

- **India.** The copy read is the NCRTC-hosted 2017 policy PDF (PDF creation date 3 May 2017; no notification date is printed in the text read) [^S-bench-pop-001]. The Mumbai–Ahmedabad HSR station-area project was signed with JICA on 12 October 2022 as a technical cooperation project, with a duration of 48 months [^S-bench-pop-005] [^S-bench-pop-006]. Its aim is planning capacity for state, municipal and NHSRCL bodies; the press release describes no construction, land acquisition or financing scheme [^S-bench-pop-005]. The corridor's current construction status was not checked in this session.
- **Indonesia (Jakarta–Bandung HSR, Whoosh).** Commercial launch in October 2023 [^S-bench-pop-067]. The operator is PT Kereta Cepat Indonesia-China (KCIC), a joint venture between Indonesian and Chinese enterprises [^S-bench-pop-035]. Cost: the consortium put the total at 113 trillion rupiah (USD 7.36 billion) after an agreed overrun (2023 statement) [^S-bench-pop-034]. The previous estimate was described as "$2 billion over budget" [^S-bench-pop-074]; the agreed overrun was US$1.2 billion [^S-bench-pop-033].
- **Hong Kong (MTR).** The 2025 annual report shows total EBIT of HK$21,983 million [^S-bench-pop-069] of which Hong Kong property development EBIT was HK$13,221 million, printed as 60.1% of total EBIT [^S-bench-pop-070]. The report's EBIT line for Hong Kong transport operations is HK$(254) million in 2025 against HK$(63) million in 2024 [^S-bench-pop-008].
- **Singapore.** The Ministry of Transport reply of 1 August 2022 (as extracted) states covered linkways are provided within a 400 m radius of major transport nodes [^S-bench-pop-024]. No primary URA TOD guideline was read.
- **Japan (Toyama).** The city's Portram LRT began operation in 2006 as the first full-scale LRT in Japan [^S-bench-pop-068]; the Centram line followed in 2009 and the North-South connection was completed in 2020 [^S-bench-pop-013] [^S-bench-pop-014]. The city had about 420,000 people in the World Bank's description [^S-bench-pop-079] and a density of about 330 people per km2 in the 2000s [^S-bench-pop-015].
- **South Korea.** The KTX Honam line's opening date and station effects were searched; the KOTI slide deck that gives a 2014 opening was seen only in a search snippet and is not used [^S-bench-pop-047].
- **Germany.** The federal status report of May 2007 is titled as the "5 Jahre" (five years) review of Stadtumbau Ost [^S-bench-pop-072]. The programme was for towns in eastern Länder [^S-bench-pop-029]; the report says three quarters of East German towns above 10,000 inhabitants took part [^S-bench-pop-029].

That compares with the predecessor line, not a forecast. The Centram project is reported to have turned a tram line from losing passengers at 3.2% a year to increasing at 4% a year [^S-bench-pop-073]. These are city data as reproduced by the World Bank.

## 5. Thresholds: density, city size and walking distance

- **India (grade A).** Influence zone: "within the walking distance of (500-800 m)" [^S-bench-pop-001]. Minimum FAR: "the minimum FAR should be 300 - 500%, and can be higher, depending on the city size" [^S-bench-pop-002]. Affordable housing: "a minimum percentage (30% or higher) of allowed FAR for affordable housing" [^S-bench-pop-003]. These are policy norms. FAR is a density proxy (floor area per plot), not population density. No outcome data tests them.
- **Japan (grade A).** A fixed-rate subsidy for Location Optimisation Plans applies to cities with "人口10万人未満かつ人口減少率が20%以上" (population under 100,000 and population decline of 20% or more) [^S-bench-pop-022]. Plans must set numeric targets for population density and public-transport users [^S-bench-pop-023]. The eligibility rule is a funding threshold, not evidence of an effect.
- **Germany (grade A, descriptive).** Three quarters of East German towns above 10,000 inhabitants were in the programme [^S-bench-pop-029]. This describes participation, not a rule.
- **Singapore (grade A for 400 m).** Covered linkways are provided within a 400 m radius of major transport nodes [^S-bench-pop-024]; the reply says "There are no plans to expand the current criteria of the programme" [^S-bench-pop-025]. The 800 m radius in the reply's title [^S-bench-pop-024] is the question's topic, not a planning standard in the reply. Press reports of URA site plans cite 800 m to a 10-minute walk, 500 m and 350 m [^S-bench-pop-039] [^S-bench-pop-040] [^S-bench-pop-041]; they are unverified and the 800 m figure is not established as a URA standard.
- **Density of Toyama before the compact-city policy.** About 330 people per km2 in the 2000s; the World Bank describes this as one of the lowest among prefectural capitals [^S-bench-pop-015]. Observed, not a threshold.
- **Test of the 200 to 800 km band:** not tested in this dossier. The sources read contain no mode-share-by-distance or air-rail substitution evidence for these cases, and the Jakarta–Bandung corridor's length was not sourced here. The band is tested in the country dossiers, not here.

## 7. Overruns, delays and renegotiations

- **Jakarta–Bandung (grade C).** An agreed overrun of US$1.2 billion, stated by the Deputy Minister of State-Owned Enterprises to parliament on 13 February 2023 [^S-bench-pop-033]. The consortium's total after the overrun is quoted at 113 trillion rupiah (USD 7.36 billion) [^S-bench-pop-034]. The earlier estimate was "$2 billion over budget" [^S-bench-pop-074]. **The source gives no pre-overrun baseline, so no outturn-to-baseline ratio is computed** and the overruns file is header-only. Press in search results describes audit rounds of about US$1.2 billion (Q1 2022) and more than US$1.4 billion (Q3 2022) [^S-bench-pop-088]; unverified, not used. The origin of the two overrun figures ($2 billion versus $1.2 billion agreed) is not reconciled by the sources read. The Jakarta Post coverage was not opened in full.
- **Toyama LRT (grade C).** The city built the first full-scale LRT in about three years [^S-bench-pop-019], where the World Bank says typical LRT construction took six to nine years [^S-bench-pop-083]. Reusing old tracks "reduced initial investments by nearly 75 percent compared with constructing new LRT tracks" [^S-bench-pop-075]. The World Bank says that using existing tracks improved NPV by JPY 16.9 billion [^S-bench-pop-081] and a six-year shorter build by JPY 21.6 billion [^S-bench-pop-082]; without the old tracks and with a nine-year build, the NPV could have been negative at JPY 13.6 billion [^S-bench-pop-080]. The NPV figures are modelled, not outturn.
- **India, Singapore, Türkiye, Germany, South Korea:** no overrun or renegotiation figure read. No reliable data found (searched: WebSearch on MAHSR cost and NHSRCL, 31 Oct 2026; the NHSRCL and JICA project-cost documents were not reached).

## 8. Evidence on towns and housing: did stations and rail-linked policy change towns?

This section lists each study with method, sample and grade. Where evidence is mixed or negative, it is said plainly.

### 8.1 General rail and property values (peer-reviewed meta-analysis, abstract only)
- Mohammad, Graham, Melo and Anderson (2013), *Transportation Research Part A* 50: 158–170. Method: meta-analysis of "23 studies (102 observations)" of rail impacts on land and property values [^S-bench-pop-038]. The abstract reports that estimated impacts vary widely, and that "a number of factors produce significant variation" (the repository record cuts the abstract off there). Grade B (peer-reviewed; abstract read only, journal paywalled). Finding: effects are variable across studies, so a single distance or uplift figure should not be carried across cities. A 500–800 m band attributed to this paper appears only on an unreviewed wiki page [^S-bench-pop-085] and is not used.

### 8.2 Toyama (grade C; World Bank case citing Toyama City data; no counterfactual)
- Method: city statistics on net migration and land prices, reproduced by the World Bank in 2021; no control areas. Net migration into the city centre "turned positive in 2008 in the city" and into the LRT corridors in 2012 [^S-bench-pop-020]. Land prices "were decreasing by 2 to 3 percent per annum until around 2012, after which land prices began to increase at 1 to 2 percent per annum" [^S-bench-pop-021]. The World Bank summary says the compact-city initiative "reversed negative trends in net migration and land prices along LRT corridors" [^S-bench-pop-009].
- Direction: positive in the city's own data, consistent over the period. Limits: the figures are the city's, there is no comparison with other Japanese cities that did not build an LRT, and the World Bank itself says "other benefits were difficult to measure in quantitative terms" [^S-bench-pop-076]. The World Bank records the mayor acknowledging that the LRT is likely to lose money every year [^S-bench-pop-018]. This is a case, not an evaluation.
- Town size: the city had about 420,000 people [^S-bench-pop-079].

### 8.3 South Korea: KTX and population redistribution (grade C; abstract only)
- Method: "multiple regression analysis" on 2000–2016 population movement data (abstract) [^S-bench-pop-031]. Finding: net migration influx rose by 3.70% after KTX began [^S-bench-pop-031]; the abstract reports larger rises where the station was outside the Seoul Metropolitan Area (12.37%) [^S-bench-pop-032] and in a non-urban area an hour or more away (5.96%) [^S-bench-pop-071]. The abstract does not describe the sample by region, and the journal and peer-review status are not on the repository record.
- Mixed: a 2010 Korean paper in the "straw effect" tradition is reported in search results as finding that KTX drew population towards Seoul [^S-bench-pop-086]; this is unverified (not opened). The direction of KTX's effect on regional population is therefore contested in the Korean literature as far as the sources read show. No Honam-line station-level result was read.

### 8.4 Germany: Stadtumbau Ost (grade A; federal status report based on a municipal survey)
- Method: survey of the participating municipalities, reported in May 2007 as a five-year interim review [^S-bench-pop-072]. Demolition: a total of 193,302 dwelling units (printed 193.302; to 28 February 2007; eastern Länder and Berlin), of which 23,066 from Land programmes and 170,236 from the federal-Länder programme (table total, printed "Summe 23.066 170.236 193.302") [^S-bench-pop-026]. Vacancy: the vacancy rate fell "um etwa 1,4 %" between 2002 and 2005 (printed as %; the report's comparison) [^S-bench-pop-027]. Stock in programme towns fell "um etwa 3 %" over the same period, partly on estimated figures [^S-bench-pop-077].
- Counterfactual, from a housing association's member data (cited in the report): without demolitions, vacancy at member companies "voraussichtlich auf 18,7 % gestiegen" (about 18.7%) in 2005 [^S-bench-pop-028]. That is the association's estimate, not an audit.
- Mixed and negative: the report itself says the desired effects "insbesondere in den Altbau- quartieren noch nicht überall zum Tragen kommen" (are not yet everywhere, in particular in old-building quarters) [^S-bench-pop-078].
- Rail: the report names "ungenutztem Bahngelände" (unused railway land) as a source of "starke Umfeldbeeinträchtigungen" (strong negative effects on the surroundings) [^S-bench-pop-030]. This is a statement about brownfield blight. The report does not measure any rail station's effect on housing.

### 8.5 Hong Kong: rail-plus-property financials (grade A; operator accounts, not a town-outcome study)
- In 2025, Hong Kong property development EBIT was HK$13,221 million, printed as 60.1% of the group's total EBIT of HK$21,983 million [^S-bench-pop-070] [^S-bench-pop-069]; Hong Kong transport operations EBIT was HK$(254) million [^S-bench-pop-008]. This is evidence that the operator's model depends on property, not evidence about towns. The wording "rail plus property" as a funding model was not verified in the PDF extract read.

### 8.6 Japan: Tokyu Tama Den-en-toshi (lead only)
- The private rail-plus-land-readjustment model in Tama is the classic Japanese example. The sources found in search (a Tokyu presentation, a PPIAF toolkit case [^S-bench-pop-048], a J-STAGE preprint) were not read in full: the PPIAF PDF download returned an HTML error page. No outcome figure is used. No reliable data found for Tama's ridership or land-value outcomes (searched: WebSearch in English, 31 Oct 2026).

### 8.7 India, Indonesia (Tegalluar, Padalarang), Singapore, Türkiye
- **India:** the policy is a norm; the MAHSR station-area work started in 2022; no outcome data. No reliable data found on land values or housing near MAHSR stations.
- **Indonesia:** TOD at Tegalluar (Bandung Regency) and access works at Padalarang are reported in academic and press sources (flooding and the access road are mentioned [^S-bench-pop-087]); these were seen only in search results. No reliable data found on TOD outcomes (searched: WebSearch, 31 Oct 2026).
- **Singapore:** the MOT reply sets the covered-linkway radius and states no plans to expand its criteria [^S-bench-pop-025]. No outcome evaluation read.
- **Türkiye:** a TOKİ and station-area search returned urban renewal projects with no station link. No reliable data found (searched: Turkish-language WebSearch, 31 Oct 2026).

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- The sources read tie station-area planning to numeric rules: walking radii (India 500–800 m [^S-bench-pop-001]), FAR (India 300–500% [^S-bench-pop-002]), affordable-housing shares (India 30% of FAR or higher [^S-bench-pop-003]), funding eligibility (Japan under 100,000 people and at least a 20% decline [^S-bench-pop-022]), and covered walkways (Singapore 400 m [^S-bench-pop-024]). These are rules and intentions. None of the sources read reports a measured effect of a rule on town population or housing.
- Outcome evidence in these sources is thin. It consists of one city-produced case (Toyama: positive net migration and land-price trends since about 2008–2012 [^S-bench-pop-020] [^S-bench-pop-021], with no counterfactual, and an acknowledged operating loss), one Korean regression abstract (positive migration effects, with an opposite-direction literature reported but not read), and a German federal status report on demolition (counts, and self-reported vacancy). The evidence suggests the effects are positive in some places and contested in others.
- Delivery choice mattered in the cases read: Toyama's reuse of old tracks is reported to have cut initial investment by nearly 75% [^S-bench-pop-075] and to have improved the appraisal [^S-bench-pop-081]. This is a single city's appraisal, not a generalisable finding.
- Forecast and overrun records are weak in the cases read. For Jakarta–Bandung, the ridership forecast and the pre-overrun baseline are not in any source read, so neither a ridership-against-forecast ratio nor an overrun percentage can be stated.
- The question whether rail-linked housing changes the population of a shrinking city is not answered by these sources. Toyama is the closest case and is a single city with city-produced data.

## Escalations

1. **Jakarta–Bandung: conflicting and missing figures.** Press forecasts (31,000 / 44,000 / 60,000 a day) [^S-bench-pop-042] [^S-bench-pop-044] [^S-bench-pop-043] conflict and no primary forecast was read; the overrun is "$2 billion" (earlier) [^S-bench-pop-074] and US$1.2 billion (agreed) [^S-bench-pop-033] with no printed baseline. Both are recorded; nothing chosen. Needs KCIC or Indonesian ministry documents (coordinator).
2. **India: publisher and primary.** The policy text was read on the NCRTC-hosted copy; the MoHUA original was not reached and the MoHUA attribution is from search. Needs verification against mohua.gov.in before any citation outside this dossier.
3. **Toyama: weight of evidence.** All Toyama outcome figures are city data reproduced by the World Bank (grade C). No independent evaluation was found. Whether this counts as CP1 evidence for station-area housing is a coordinator judgement.
4. **Singapore 800 m.** The 800 m figure is press only; the MOT primary gives 400 m for covered linkways. The conflict is not resolved; URA primary needed.
5. **MTR "Rail plus Property" wording.** Seen in a snippet, not in the E07 extract. Needs the full Business Review text.
6. **Korean KTX paper.** Journal and peer-review status unknown (repository record only). Needs the full paper.
7. **National series hides regional decline.** Germany's national total rose while eastern towns shrank [^S-bench-pop-065] [^S-bench-pop-066]; Korea's national total rose slightly [^S-bench-pop-063] [^S-bench-pop-064], with regional decline in the cited literature. Regional series (Destatis, BBSR, KOSIS) not read.
8. **METHOD v1.2 changed during the run.** The cost-level classification applies to cost_per_km rows; this dossier has none, so the classification is not applied. No other method change affected this dossier.
