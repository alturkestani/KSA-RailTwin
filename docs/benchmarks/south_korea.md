# South Korea: KTX (Korea Train Express, operated by Korail; Gyeongbu and Honam lines)

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026. Figures below were read in this session on the page or PDF named in their footnote. Three exceptions are marked in the text: "press" (a newspaper or trade article that names its audit or operator source), "abstract only" (a peer-reviewed paper read through its abstract, full text paywalled) and "unverified" (search snippet, or a page that could not be read). Figures are shown as printed, in the currency and price basis of each source. No price conversion is made here; converted values: see README.md. Metric rows are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-south_korea.csv` (nominal values as printed, unit changes only where noted); overrun candidates are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-south_korea.csv`. The main evidence base is the 2005 Korean Society for Railway paper by Oh In-Tack (grade C: proceedings, with KORAIL and KOTI data as cited), six peer-reviewed abstracts (grade B), the Korea Transport Institute (KOTI) route page (grade C) and 1998 press reporting of a Board of Audit and Inspection (BAI) review (grade C). The BAI report itself was not reached. The second operator (SRT) was not researched in this pass. Escalations are at the end.

## 3. Ridership vs forecast

Forecast basis. The forecast for the Gyeongbu corridor was the 2003 KRRI study; the paper reports its 2004 converted KTX demand as 139 thousand persons per day [^S-bench-kr-001]. Forecast traffic demand for 2004 on the Gyeongbu line fell from 260 thousand to 139 thousand persons per day across the plan studies, as the phasing changed [^S-bench-kr-040].

Outturn, first year (April 2004 to March 2005). KTX Gyeongbu averaged 62 thousand persons per day [^S-bench-kr-002], or 22.743 million passengers over 12 months [^S-bench-kr-039]. Against the 139 thousand forecast this is a ratio of 0.446 (computed; metric M-kr-013, overrun OV-kr-002) [^S-bench-kr-001][^S-bench-kr-002]. Total rail (Gyeongbu and Honam) averaged 153 thousand per day against a 314 thousand forecast, printed as 48.7% [^S-bench-kr-003].

Abstracts. Lee and Chang (2006): "actual ridership rates have been approximately half those originally estimated" [^S-bench-kr-009]. Chang and Lee (2008): "approximately half of that forecast" [^S-bench-kr-010]. Suh and Yang (2005): the numbers "represent only about half the demand forecasted" in the first months [^S-bench-kr-011]. The same Suh and Yang abstract reports one million passengers in 14 days and 10 million in 142 days [^S-bench-kr-014].

Station pairs, Q2 2004 (one quarter only). Seoul-origin KTX demand against the 2004 forecast, as printed in the 2005 paper's Table 8: Seoul-Busan 37.1%; Seoul-Cheonan-Asan 90.4%; Seoul-Daejeon 25.8%; Seoul-Dongdaegu 53.1% [^S-bench-kr-004] (metric rows M-kr-014 to M-kr-017; overruns OV-kr-004 to OV-kr-007). Cheonan-Asan, the intermediate station nearest to Seoul, was the pair closest to forecast; Daejeon was the furthest.

Explanations offered in the sources (as the authors state them). The 2005 paper attributes the gap mainly to trip-generation assumptions that tied travel to population and regional output, which it says were not supported by the observed decline in passenger trips since 1992, and to incomplete transfer modelling [^S-bench-kr-001][^S-bench-kr-040]. It also names the limit on services (planned 164-184 services, reduced to 132-152 by October 2005) [^S-bench-kr-007], the seat layout and interior complaints from the start [^S-bench-kr-045], and the phasing that cut the Busan leg's time saving [^S-bench-kr-008]. The paper's own wording is that these are the causes it "identifies" or "estimates"; no causal test is run for each.

Ex-post at five years or later. No reliable data found for KTX ridership in the years after 2005 in the sources opened here (searched: the KORAIL section-statistics file on data.go.kr (page opened, XLSX not downloadable) [^S-bench-kr-042]; the 2005 KSR paper; Lee and Chang 2006; Chang and Lee 2008). The press figure for 2024 (KTX passengers: "8000만명이 넘어섰지만", i.e. more than 80 million) appears in the Bizwatch article; it is grade C and is not a forecast comparison [^S-bench-kr-036].

Reading. Within the sources read, the first-year KTX outturn was about half of the 2004 forecast on the Gyeongbu corridor, and the corridor-wide ratios all sit near one half. The one exception is the Seoul-Cheonan-Asan pair (90.4%) [^S-bench-kr-004]. These sources are all from 2005-2008; no later evaluation was found.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes and the 200-800 km band

Density and city-size thresholds. No reliable data found (searched: Korean and English queries on high-speed rail thresholds, density and city size; the sources read give no density or city-size threshold).

The 200-800 km band (hypothesis, tested here only for the Korean corridor). The Korean corridor data span about 97 to 444 km (corridor distances as printed by the 2005 paper; the paper does not state whether they are road or rail distances) [^S-bench-kr-005]. No Korean HSR corridor pair above 444 km was measured in the sources read.

Rail share by distance, all intercity trips, as printed. Before opening (2003) and in Q2 2004 [^S-bench-kr-005]:
- Seoul-Cheonan, 97.1 km: 23.9% (2003), 23.5% (2004 Q2). For this pair the paper's text says the share fell slightly ("약간 줄었지만", section 4.2(2)) [^S-bench-kr-005].
- Seoul-Daejeon, 166.8 km: 24.4% (2003), 34.9% (2004 Q2) [^S-bench-kr-005].
- Seoul-Dongdaegu, 327.1 km: 47.8% (2003), 72.8% (2004 Q2) [M-kr-023, M-kr-025].
- Seoul-Busan, 444.3 km: 37.5% (2003), 60.4% (2004 Q2) [M-kr-024, M-kr-026].

Air share. Air's share of the Seoul-Busan trips fell from 41.7% (2003) to 25.5% (2004 Q2); Seoul-Dongdaegu air fell from 17.1% to 3.9% [^S-bench-kr-005]. Park and Ha (2006) report that domestic aviation demand in Korea fell "between 34% and 75%", and attribute the fall to highway openings and to the KTX Seoul-Daegu service [^S-bench-kr-017]. Their stated-preference survey was run 8 months before the opening [^S-bench-kr-017]. The two causes cannot be separated in the abstract.

Car and rail by distance. Lee and Chang (2006) report that "the automobile is a significant travel mode for trips of short to medium distances" and that KTX is the major mode for medium-to-long trips [^S-bench-kr-016]. Their abstract is the only source read that states the distance pattern in words.

Air-to-KTX conversion (2004 Q2 estimate, the paper's own method). Estimated conversion from air to KTX was 47.3%, against 70.0% from Saemaeul and 10.6% from car [^S-bench-kr-008].

Test result. Within the sampled range (about 300 to 450 km), the Korean data show rail share rising and air share falling, with the largest rise at 327 km [^S-bench-kr-005]. Within the 97-167 km part of the sample, the rise is smaller or absent (Cheonan) or moderate (Daejeon). The data do not show a shorter-distance cut-off, and the sampled corridor reaches only 444 km, so the data neither confirm nor reject a 200-800 km band, and say nothing about distances above 450 km. The Seoul-Busan rail share (60.4%) was below Seoul-Daegu (72.8%) in the same quarter, so the share is not monotonic in distance in these data.

## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

Planned station catchments. The 2003 forecast used seven catchment zones on the Gyeongbu corridor: Seoul, Gwangmyeong, Cheonan-Asan, Daejeon, Dongdaegu, Gyeongju and Busan [^S-bench-kr-006] (metric M-kr-027). These are forecast zones, not an operating stop list. Ulsan and Osong are not separate zones in the table.

Service levels. The paper reports that planned high-speed services of 164-184 were reduced to 132-152 by October 2005, with 46 trainsets (the paper's account; the time unit is not stated in the sentence) [^S-bench-kr-007][^S-bench-kr-028]. The BAI press report says the cost estimate assumed 46 trainsets while the demand figure needed 63 a day [^S-bench-kr-028].

Travel time. Seoul-Busan travel time is printed as 2 hours 29 minutes at up to 300 km/h [^S-bench-kr-035].

Station spacing. No reliable data found (searched: the KOTI English route page gives route lengths and phase periods but no station distances [^S-bench-kr-030][^S-bench-kr-033]; encyclopedia snippets were not opened).

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Cost (plan against audit projection). The government's second revised plan (announced November 1997) gave 17,502,800 million won to 2005 [^S-bench-kr-022]. The BAI, as reported in April 1998, added 4,526,400 million won and projected 22,029,200 million won, a projected increase of 25.86% on the plan (computed; metric M-kr-010; overrun OV-kr-001) [^S-bench-kr-023]. This is a projection by the audit, not an outturn; the press says the additions were omitted items, including a new Seoul-Namseoul line, extra rolling stock and depot expansion [^S-bench-kr-023]. The BAI also said the financing plan was at risk: the planned bond interest of 8-12% was in doubt [^S-bench-kr-023]. The government's B/C ratio was 1.21, and BAI said it could fall to between 1.11 and 0.86 [^S-bench-kr-024][^S-bench-kr-029].

Delays (BAI, as reported). The press describes delays from a Biryong bridge safety question that was unresolved for over three years, and from a two-year decision on station finishes [^S-bench-kr-023]. It also reports 100 or more skilled staff leaving between 1996 and 1997 and the project relying on outside contractors [^S-bench-kr-023].

Over-design. BAI, as reported, found the Cheonan station designed for about eight times the 2028 forecast of 7,100 persons per day, with 26.7 billion won at risk [^S-bench-kr-025]. The 1998 article says the BAI-reported demand basis for the trainsets (63 a day) was not matched by the 46 trainsets in the cost estimate [^S-bench-kr-028].

Schedule. Phase 1 opened on 1 April 2004 [^S-bench-kr-013]. KOTI's construction period for Phase 1 is printed as 1992-2004 [^S-bench-kr-034]. No reliable data found for the original baseline date of Phase 1 in the sources opened (the 1998 article discusses alternative construction scenarios, not a baseline date). For Phase 2, the 2005 abstract gives a planned completion of 2010 [^S-bench-kr-015]. A UIC eNews snippet gives 1 November 2010 for the Daegu-Busan section, but the page could not be read and the text is not in the page fetched, so it is unverified and not used [^S-bench-kr-041]. No schedule overrun is therefore computed.

Renegotiation and restructuring. No reliable data found on renegotiation of the Gyeongbu contracts in the sources opened.

Honam. No reliable data found on Honam overruns or the original budget (see section 10).

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

Summary of the empirical studies read (abstracts only except as noted). Evidence is mixed. The clearest studies are on accessibility and migration, and they point in different directions.

1. Heo, Kim and Yoo (2018), 국토계획 (Journal of the Korea Planning Association) 53(4), pp. 107-122 (grade B; abstract only). Method: multiple regression on 2000-2016 municipal population movement data, with KTX-opening and station-location terms. Finding as printed: "the net migrating population influx had risen by 3.70% after the beginning of the KTX operation" [^S-bench-kr-019]. Station location: "higher by 12.37%" for stations outside the Seoul Metropolitan Area, "by 5.96%" for non-urban stations one hour or more from Seoul Station, and "by 2.91%" for innovation-designated cities [^S-bench-kr-020]. Direction: in-migration rose more where stations were outside the capital area and in non-urban places (the study's reading: this points to a mitigation of concentration). Limits: observational regression, not a station-level causal design; the abstract does not say how many stations or towns were in the sample.

2. Heo (2010), Journal of Urban Administration 23(4), pp. 59-74 (grade C; review article; abstract only). Method: critical review of indicators and existing studies. Finding: "the straw effects of KTX have ... no acceptable evidence so far" [^S-bench-kr-021]. Direction: negative for the straw effect (no evidence). Note: same first author as study 1 (2018), which reaches a different reading on the later data.

3. Kim and Sultana (2015), Journal of Transport Geography 45, pp. 48-61 (grade B; abstract only, via RePEc/IDEAS). Method: weighted average travel time and potential accessibility indicators, stage by stage, 2004-2018. Finding: "the spatial equity is degenerated by the extension in 2010/2011 as the improvement of accessibility has been concentrated in cities along the primary HSR corridor near the already-advantageous Seoul capital area" [^S-bench-kr-018]. Accessibility widened to more cities but concentrated along the corridor; the authors say the 2018 extension would help isolated regions, but "not large enough" without more inter-provincial links [^S-bench-kr-018]. Direction: mixed. Accessibility gains for intermediate and corridor cities are reported; equity gains for peripheral regions are reported as limited.

4. Chang and Lee (2008), Transport Reviews 28(1), pp. 87-103 (grade B; abstract only). Method: reduced Hansen-type accessibility measure, ANOVA and GIS mapping for the Seoul metropolitan area. Finding: "patronage, however, is approximately half of that forecast" [^S-bench-kr-010]. The authors identify "zones of opportunity" and recommend metropolitan rail expansion to improve access [^S-bench-kr-010]. Direction: concerns the Seoul area, not towns on the corridor.

5. Oh In-Tack (2005), KSR proceedings (grade C; forecast-to-outturn tables). Station-level result: Seoul-Cheonan-Asan was 90.4% of forecast, Seoul-Daejeon 25.8% [^S-bench-kr-004]. The Cheonan-Asan rail share fell slightly (23.9% to 23.5%), while Seoul-Daejeon rail share rose from 24.4% to 34.9% [^S-bench-kr-005]. Direction: mixed; the paper does not test town-level outcomes (jobs, retail, population) and covers one quarter.

Station-area outcomes (Cheonan-Asan and others). No reliable data found on the economic outcome of station areas in town centres (searched: Korean-language queries on KTX station-area development and vacancy; the results read were property and marketing items, not evaluations). The only Cheonan-Asan item with a figure is the BAI over-design finding for the station building [^S-bench-kr-025].

Peripheral station evidence. The sources read do not test non-town or out-of-town stations as a category, apart from the Heo et al. 2018 station-location terms [^S-bench-kr-020].

Overall reading. The evidence on intermediate stations is mixed: one grade B regression (2018) reports more in-migration into non-capital and non-urban station places; one review (2010) finds no acceptable straw-effect evidence; one grade B accessibility study (2015) finds equity gains concentrated along the corridor; and the one station-level forecast table (2005) finds a single pair (Cheonan-Asan) close to forecast and Daejeon well short of it. No study read shows a clear net gain in jobs or population for a town that was not already a city. The Japanese and Spanish intermediate-station studies are in docs/benchmarks/japan.md and docs/benchmarks/spain.md and are not repeated here.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

The sources read point to the following, each stated as the source reports it. These are not recommendations for any KSA line.

- Demand. Most of the Korean ratios read sit near one half of the forecast in the first year or first months (abstracts; corridor totals) [^S-bench-kr-009][^S-bench-kr-010][^S-bench-kr-011][^S-bench-kr-003]; the Seoul-Cheonan-Asan pair (90.4%) is the exception [^S-bench-kr-004]. The 2005 paper attributes the gap to trip-generation assumptions and transfer modelling [^S-bench-kr-001]. The first-year figures come from a single corridor.
- Distance and mode. In the 2004 Q2 data, rail's share of intercity trips on the 327 km and 444 km corridor pairs was 72.8% and 60.4%, with air losing share [^S-bench-kr-005]. The Korean evidence is for these distances and one quarter; it does not test a band.
- Capacity and sizing. The BAI-reported cost estimate used fewer trainsets (46) than the demand estimate needed (63), and the Cheonan station was sized for about eight times the forecast 2028 demand [^S-bench-kr-028][^S-bench-kr-025]. The 2005 paper calls the dense seating an "unavoidable choice" made because demand had been over-forecast [^S-bench-kr-045]; the sources read link these choices to the forecast, and do not test the alternatives.
- Cost basis. The BAI projection was 25.86% above the 1997 plan [^S-bench-kr-022][^S-bench-kr-023]; the B/C ratio was reported as 1.21 by the government and 0.86 to 1.11 under BAI sensitivities [^S-bench-kr-024][^S-bench-kr-029].
- Intermediate stations. The evidence is mixed (section 8). Accessibility improvements were concentrated along the corridor in the 2010/2011 extension [^S-bench-kr-018].
- Financing. The 1997 plan relied on 55% bonds, which the BAI judged a risk [^S-bench-kr-026][^S-bench-kr-023]. Later operating losses are reported by press [^S-bench-kr-038] but the ratio is not verified (section 4).

## Escalations

E1. Gyeongbu total cost does not reconcile. KOTI prints 20 billion USD with no date or price basis [^S-bench-kr-031]. The 1997 government plan is 17,502,800 million won [^S-bench-kr-022] and the 1998 BAI projection is 22,029,200 million won [^S-bench-kr-023]. The three are different scopes and price bases and cannot be converted here. Needs the BAI 1998 report and the KR outturn to close.

E2. Gyeongbu route length conflict: 417.5 km (KOTI) [^S-bench-kr-030] against 423.8 km in an unopened encyclopedia snippet [^S-bench-kr-044]. The 444.3 km used in the 2005 paper for Seoul-Busan is a study corridor distance, not a rail length. The KR or MOLIT route length is needed.

E3. KORAIL 2024 operating result. Press gives revenue of 2조5483억원 and an operating loss of 1114억원 [^S-bench-kr-036][^S-bench-kr-037] with the scope unclear. An operating ratio is therefore not computed. The KORAIL annual management disclosure (ALIO) is needed.

E4. Rail and air causation. Park and Ha (2006) attribute the 34-75% aviation fall to highways and KTX together [^S-bench-kr-017]. The rail share and air share changes in the 2005 paper [^S-bench-kr-005] are for one quarter and do not separate the causes. The KDI difference-in-differences work (WP14-14) would address this and was not read.

E5. Straw effect: conflicting readings from the same first author. Heo (2010) finds no acceptable evidence of a straw effect [^S-bench-kr-021]; Heo, Kim and Yoo (2018) find larger in-migration into non-capital and non-urban station places [^S-bench-kr-019][^S-bench-kr-020]. Both are reported as published. The synthesis is for the coordinator (different data periods and measures).

E6. Ridership ratios in the sources are mostly "approximately half" in words (2006, 2008, 2005 abstracts). Their underlying numbers are not printed in the abstracts, so the verbal "half" is recorded as a grade B ratio of 0.5 with that caveat (metric rows M-kr-018 to M-kr-020). The 2005 paper gives 48.7% in print and 0.446 as a computed ratio, both grade C. The gap is consistent but not checked against each study's data.

E7. Phase 2 opening date. The UIC eNews snippet gives 1 November 2010 [^S-bench-kr-041] and the 2005 abstract planned completion for 2010 [^S-bench-kr-015]. The UIC text was not read, so no schedule overrun is computed. Needs the KORAIL or MOLIT opening notice.
