# Egypt: high-speed rail under construction (Green Line Ain Sokhna–Alexandria–Marsa Matrouh; Blue Line Cairo–Aswan; Red Line Hurghada–Safaga–Luxor)

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026 by the R1b collector. Egypt's high-speed network is under construction and NOT operating: no passenger service is recorded in any source read, so ridership, farebox and operating-ratio outturns are "no reliable data found (not operating)". Every figure below was read on a page named in its footnote, or is marked "secondary" (Wikipedia) or "press" (grade C or D as in the metric file). Figures are nominal, in the currency of the source. Conversion to SAR 2025 is generated in `README.md` (converted values: see README.md). Metric rows are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-egypt.csv` and overrun candidates in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-egypt.csv`. Escalations are listed at the end.

Evidence base in one line. The contract values and the 2021–2022 scope come from Siemens Mobility's own press releases (grade A). The schedule baselines come from Egyptian Ministry of Transport statements relayed on a Cairo government news site (grade A, relayed) and later dates from press reports of ministerial statements (grade C). No Egyptian government audit, NAT annual report, Ministry of Finance statement or peer-reviewed study was reached. The Egyptian government site sis.gov.eg was not reached in this session, and the Ahram Online article pages returned HTTP 403.

Naming. "Green Line" is the name Siemens and the German export credit agency use for the first line (Ain Sokhna–Alexandria–Marsa Matrouh, 660 km [^S-bench-eg-013]). The Blue and Red lines are named in the press; no primary Blue or Red line statement was opened.

## 3. Ridership vs forecast (forecast at decision, outcome at +5 years; ratio)

No reliable data found (not operating). No outturn ridership exists. The ratio "outturn / forecast" cannot be computed.

Announced forecast (not an outturn): the first line is reported to be used by 30 million passengers a year, with no forecast year and no named source [^S-bench-eg-034]. Grade D (trade press). To verify against Siemens, NAT or the Ministry.

Government capacity target (not a ridership forecast): a combined capacity of about 4 million passengers per day on railways and high-speed trains by 2030 [^S-bench-eg-031]. This is a capacity target stated by the Transport Minister in press, and its form (capacity, not demand) matters for any comparison.

Search done on 31 Oct 2026: Ministry of Transport and Cabinet statements (press relays), Siemens and EKG pages, press, IMF and Wikipedia. No ridership forecast with a year, no business case and no traffic study was found in the sources read.

## 4. Farebox recovery / operating ratio (definition used by the source)

No reliable data found (not operating). No fare schedule for Egypt's high-speed service was found in the sources read. The operator (DB International Operations with Elsewedy Electric, as reported in secondary sources) has no published operating results for this network yet.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, and the 200–800 km band (air and car test)

Density and city-size thresholds: no reliable data found (searched: Egyptian government statements, Siemens and EKG pages, Wikipedia, press, 31 Oct 2026). No Egypt-specific study of population density or city-size thresholds for high-speed rail was found.

The 200–800 km band (hypothesis, not asserted): the Green Line is 660 km long [^S-bench-eg-013], inside the band. A line length is not a trip distance, and no trip distance or journey time between city pairs was read from an official source. The Blue Line is described as over 1,100 km [^S-bench-eg-036] (secondary, grade D), above the band. Mode-share-by-distance and air–rail substitution evidence for Egypt was not found. The band can only be tested from other dossiers (for example Spain, Japan, France), not from Egypt, because Egypt has no operating line. Result: not testable for Egypt; no evidence for or against the band from this country.

## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

Station count: 21 stations on the first line [^S-bench-eg-017] [^S-bench-eg-019], with a control centre and a new maintenance depot. Naive mean spacing: 660 km / 21 stations = 31.4 km per station (computed) [^S-bench-eg-013] [^S-bench-eg-017]. This divides by all 21 stations, including the two termini, and it is not a timetable or stopping-pattern statistic.

Stopping pattern: a split between regional and high-speed stations is described in a press article that was not readable (HTTP 403) and was seen only in a search summary. Not recorded as a figure. Named stops on the Green Line in secondary sources include Al-Alamein, Borg El Arab, Wadi El Natroun, the 6th October City, southern Cairo, the New Administrative Capital and Ain Sokhna (Wikipedia, grade D; not used as figures). Timetable: no reliable data found (no timetable published; trial and launch dates are not timetables).

Top speed: up to 250 km/h for the high-speed service [^S-bench-eg-009]. Regional trains run on the same first line (Siemens fleet of 94 Desiro high-capacity sets [^S-bench-eg-008]).

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Schedule. The opening date has been stated several times by official sources, and each later statement moved it:
- Ministry of Transport statement, relayed by cairo.gov.eg on 5 Sep 2022: opening "on 7 November" (2022) [^S-bench-eg-012].
- Minister, relayed by cairo.gov.eg on 4 Sep 2023: first train "in the second half of October 2023" [^S-bench-eg-015].
- Presidency, reported 25 Aug 2025: "first high-speed rail line in June 2026" [^S-bench-eg-021].
- Minister, statement of 30 Sep 2026, reported 2 Oct 2026: passenger operations "targeting July 2027" [^S-bench-eg-018].
- EKG project page, undated: "scheduled to go into operation in 2027" [^S-bench-eg-025].
Computed: the 7 Nov 2022 date to 1 Jul 2027 is about 4.65 years [^S-bench-eg-012] [^S-bench-eg-018]. These are current targets, not outturns. No service has opened in any source read. The slip is recorded as a candidate in `overruns-egypt.csv` (OV-eg-001 to OV-eg-003). The milestone types differ (first-train delivery, trial operation, inauguration, passenger start), so the intervals are not like-for-like.

Cost. No outturn cost exists. The contract values have moved in the sources read, but the moves are mainly scope changes and rounding, not a dated overrun on a fixed scope:
- The 2021 first-line award: USD 4.5 bn total with USD 3 bn Siemens share [^S-bench-eg-001]; the press gives USD 4.45 bn [^S-bench-eg-022]. Conflict kept (M-eg-012 and M-eg-014).
- The 2022 combined network contract: EUR 8.1 bn Siemens share for approximately 2,000 km [^S-bench-eg-004] [^S-bench-eg-006]. This is a scope increase from 660 km (first line) to about 2,000 km, not a like-for-like cost overrun, so no overrun ratio is recorded.
- A programme total of USD 23 bn for the network appears in secondary sources only [^S-bench-eg-035] (grade D; no primary read).
Cost overrun percentage: no reliable data found (no dated audit, no NAT or Ministry statement of cost-to-date, and no audit body report reached).

Renegotiations and fiscal exposure. No renegotiation of the Siemens contract was found in the sources read. The IMF press releases and the Article IV report were searched; the IMF pages returned HTTP 429 or were not readable, and no mention of the high-speed line was seen in the search summaries. No IMF or World Bank statement on the high-speed line was read.

Ridership/financing risk, not a cost overrun: no reliable data found for a debt-service or operating-subsidy figure for the line.

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

Not applicable (not operating). No intermediate station on the Egyptian network has opened, so no ex-post empirical study of intermediate-station effects exists or could exist from this network. Searched on 31 Oct 2026: the Egyptian government and press, Siemens, EKG, Wikipedia and academic search summaries. The one academic item in the search results is a single-author feasibility paper (Minia University, the International Journal of Applied Engineering and Business Studies). Its abstract was not read, and its methods (cost-benefit, multi-criteria, scenario) are ex-ante; it is not evidence of station effects and is not used. Result: no evidence, positive or negative, for Egypt.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

These are observations from the Egyptian record, not recommendations. The source reports are the basis for each point.
1. Announced schedules moved. The first-line opening was stated as 7 November 2022 [^S-bench-eg-012], then second half of October 2023 for the first train [^S-bench-eg-015], June 2026 [^S-bench-eg-021] and July 2027 [^S-bench-eg-018]. The evidence suggests that earlier official dates were not met.
2. Cost totals depend on scope and on what is included. The first-line contract includes 15 years of maintenance and rolling stock [^S-bench-eg-001] [^S-bench-eg-003]; per-km ratios from these totals are not civil-works figures [M-eg-018 to M-eg-020 in the metric file].
3. Forecasts are not published with a year or a method. The only ridership figure found is an unattributed 30 million passengers per year with no year [^S-bench-eg-034], and the government's public target is a daily capacity of about 4 million by 2030 [^S-bench-eg-031].
4. Financing was reported from export credit cover, a multilateral lender and a third-line loan from European institutions [^S-bench-eg-024] [^S-bench-eg-027] [^S-bench-eg-028]. The loan terms of the multilateral and the third-line loan are not confirmed by a primary source.
5. The network's size has been stated differently over time: 1,800 km planned in 2021 [^S-bench-eg-002], 2,000 km in 2022 [^S-bench-eg-006] and 2,250 km planned in 2026 [^S-bench-eg-032]. The line length of the first line has also varied: 660 km [^S-bench-eg-013] and 675 km [^S-bench-eg-016].

## Escalations

These are conflicts the collector could not resolve. The coordinator should decide which figure, if any, goes into the synthesis.
1. Green Line length: 660 km (Ministry statement 2022, Siemens 2021 scope, EKG page, 2026 press) versus 675 km (Minister 2023, cairo.gov.eg) [^S-bench-eg-013] [^S-bench-eg-016]. Both kept (M-eg-001, M-eg-002). The 2023 figure may include a different measurement basis; not verified.
2. First-line contract value: USD 4.5 bn (Siemens 1 Sep 2021) [^S-bench-eg-001] versus USD 4.45 bn (press, 15-year contract) [^S-bench-eg-022]. Both kept (M-eg-012, M-eg-014). Scope and rounding not reconciled.
3. Opening schedule: the baseline (7 Nov 2022, [^S-bench-eg-012]) and current target (July 2027, [^S-bench-eg-018]) are both official-statement dates, with intermediate targets (second half of Oct 2023, [^S-bench-eg-015]; June 2026, [^S-bench-eg-021]). Interval recorded as a candidate overrun; the outturn is not yet observed, so it cannot be used as a reference-class observation until the service opens. Escalate to R1c for treatment of target-only observations.
4. Network size and the Blue and Red lines: 1,800 km planned (2021) [^S-bench-eg-002], about 2,000 km under construction (2022 and 2026) [^S-bench-eg-006] [^S-bench-eg-033], 2,250 km planned (2026) [^S-bench-eg-032]. Blue Line "over 1,100 km" is secondary only [^S-bench-eg-036]. A search summary gave a different combined length for the two southern lines, not opened and not used.
5. Third line: 175 km connecting Upper Egypt governorates [^S-bench-eg-030] (press, unnamed sources). Not reconciled with the Blue Line naming in other sources. Grade D.
6. Network programme cost USD 23 bn [^S-bench-eg-035] (secondary, Wikipedia) and the per-km computation from it: grade D; no primary read. Do not use until the Global Construction Review article (cited by Wikipedia) is opened.
7. Operator contract: Deutsche Bahn with Elsewedy Electric, with a 15-year initial term reported in secondary sources [^S-bench-eg-037]; not verified from a primary source (DB press page returned HTTP 404). Coordinator to decide whether the term matters for the delivery-model comparison.
8. Scope of the EUR 8.1 bn figure: Siemens calls it the combined contract for the network [^S-bench-eg-004]. A secondary account gives a narrower scope for the same figure; that account is not recorded as a source and is not used.
9. Velaro top speed: 250 km/h (Siemens 2025) [^S-bench-eg-009]. A different top speed appears in search summaries only; not opened; not used.
