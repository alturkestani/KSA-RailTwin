# W5 benchmarks: cross-country synthesis

This note reads across the 13 dossiers, `small-towns.md` and the generated table in `README.md`. It adds no new figure: every number below is taken from a dossier, with that dossier's source footnote, and converted costs are only in `README.md` (generated by `rail.benchmarks summarise`). Observed outcomes in other countries are evidence about those countries, not forecasts for Saudi Arabia. The note states what the evidence shows and where it is thin; it does not recommend building or not building any line (rule 9).

## 1. Cost per km varies widely, within countries as much as between them
- The cost table in `README.md` spans more than an order of magnitude across the audited lines, and the spread inside one country is often as large as between countries. France's own record runs from 6.0 to 28.3 M EUR (2023 prices) per km across ten lines[^S-bench-fr-098].
- The sources name the drivers rather than measure them: urban nodes, land, viaducts and long tunnels in Germany[^S-bench-de-003]; design choices in Italy (the source authors' interpretation)[^S-bench-it-031]; standardised designs, a scale supply industry and labour costs in China, with the World Bank cautioning that transfer "depends on their individual situations"[^S-bench-cn-081].
- Comparability is limited: scope (rolling stock, stations, land, financing), price basis and terrain differ by row. The European Court of Auditors states its figures in nominal terms, so its rows are converted from the 2018 report year as a lower bound (A-bench-002). No decomposition by terrain class was found in any dossier; desert, dune and sabkha costs are not covered by these benchmarks (a gap for R1c).

## 2. Cost and schedule overruns are the common case in the audited record
- The European Court of Auditors found overruns on every audited line in its sample[^S-bench-fr-086]. Spain's audited lines and the Madrid–Barcelona contracts show line-level and contract-level overruns[^S-bench-es-001][^S-bench-es-003]. The UK National Audit Office reports that HS2 costs "doubled since 2020" and names underestimation, inefficient delivery and scope change as causes[^S-bench-caut-062]. Korea's audit board projected Gyeongbu costs 25.86 % above the 1997 plan[^S-bench-kr-022].
- Announced opening dates moved by years in Egypt[^S-bench-eg-018], Turkey (Ankara–Sivas)[^S-bench-tr-013] and Japan (Hokkaido, Chuo)[^S-bench-jp-010][^S-bench-jp-003].
- Not every record shows an overrun: Morocco's completed-cost figure equals the plan figure in the audit court report, so the overrun cannot be settled from the sources read (Morocco dossier, Escalation E1), and China's single recorded observation came in below its estimate (China dossier).
- The candidate observations are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns.csv` and listed in `README.md` for R1c. They mix baselines (decision, award, earlier estimate) and are not an uplift; R1c sets uplifts with `reference-class-forecasting`.

## 3. Ridership against forecast: thin, and mostly below forecast where measured
- Ex-post comparisons exist for few lines. Where they exist, most early-year outturns were below forecast: Korea's first-year ratios sit near one half of the forecast in the abstracts read[^S-bench-kr-009][^S-bench-kr-011]; the Channel Tunnel forecasts "largely and systematically overestimated" the market[^S-bench-caut-043]; Brightline's ridership growth was below its rating case[^S-bench-caut-036]; France's LGV SEA traffic was 4 to 10 % below the public-inquiry forecasts[^S-bench-fr-043].
- There are exceptions: LGV Est phase 1 came in 4 % above its expectation[^S-bench-fr-055], and Morocco's Al Boraq rose from 0.43 of its opening forecast in year 3 to 0.93 in year 7 (press-reported operator figures)[^S-bench-ma-026][^S-bench-ma-012].
- A recurrent finding is that the evaluation itself is missing: no route-level modal-shift survey or retrospective benefit estimate for Germany's VDE 8[^S-bench-de-041], and no ex-post financial return for LGV SEA[^S-bench-fr-033].

## 4. Farebox recovery is rarely public
Operating ratios at line level were found for Turkey only (ticket revenue covering 73–79 % of cost in 2017–2018, with public service payments)[^S-bench-tr-003]; Spain's operators ran operating losses in 2023–2024 on the regulator's totals[^S-bench-es-005]; China's weaker 200–250 km/h lines are linked by the World Bank to fixed, low fares[^S-bench-cn-084]. For France, Japan, Germany, Italy, Korea, Morocco, the UAE and the cautionary cases, no line-level ratio was found (README gaps table).

## 5. The 200–800 km band: a hypothesis with partial support
- Consistent with the band: in Japan rail carried the largest share in the 300–700 km bands in 2000, with air dominant beyond about 700 km[^S-bench-jp-001]; in Spain rail's share was high on 365–621 km corridors and fell sharply beyond about 180 minutes[^S-bench-es-005][^S-bench-es-004]; in Korea rail took most intercity trips on 327 km and 444 km pairs in 2004[^S-bench-kr-005]; French evidence puts air dominance at about three to four hours of rail journey[^S-bench-fr-045].
- Qualifying it: the evidence points to travel time, frequency and price rather than distance as the operative variable (Spain, France); on Berlin–Munich the European Court of Auditors found air faster door-to-door, with rail cheaper[^S-bench-de-019]; in China car and bus dominate below about 150 km[^S-bench-cn-009], which bounds the lower end only loosely; the upper bound above 800 km is not tested by any dossier's data.
- Not tested: no Gulf or desert-climate evidence (the UAE lines are short or freight-led; Egypt is not operating). The band remains a hypothesis for W8 to test with KSA skims.

## 6. Small towns and intermediate stations: mixed to negative
The cross-cutting review (`small-towns.md`) finds the clearest audit and peer-reviewed results on in-between and peripheral places negative or null: no clear regeneration at 15 of 18 audited EU stations[^S-bench-st-014], a sizable employment decrease in in-between Shinkansen municipalities[^S-bench-st-001], and GDP falls in peripheral Chinese counties on upgraded lines[^S-bench-st-032]. Positive results exist but are narrower (GDP, prices or migration, at specific stations). No study read measures a small town's population, jobs or land value against a matched control in a form that supports a planning number.

## 7. Density and catchment tests used by others
The World Bank's China model shows network returns falling sharply with density (China-specific)[^S-bench-cn-021]; the European Court of Auditors uses a nine-million-passengers-a-year benchmark and station catchment populations as audit tests[^S-bench-es-001]. No country dossier found an explicit density or city-size threshold used to decide a line or a stop.

## 8. Delivery models observed
State-led construction and operation (China, Spain's ADIF and Renfe, Turkey, Morocco, Egypt with an export-credit-financed contractor consortium), concession (France's LGV SEA; Taiwan's BOT), private (Brightline), open-access competition on public infrastructure (Italy) and a state developer with an operating joint venture (UAE). The dossiers record what each model allocated (for example, the SEA concession shifted construction-cost risk away from the public budget[^S-bench-fr-032]) but contain no like-for-like comparison of outcomes by model; W6 (R1d, R7) takes this up.

## 9. Population-growth peers
Station-area policies in the peers are mostly rules and intentions (walking radii, floor-area ratios, affordable-housing shares, funding eligibility), not measured outcomes (`population_growth_peers.md`). Toyama is the closest outcome case and is a single city with city-produced data and no counterfactual.

## 10. Main gaps carried forward
Line-level farebox and operating ratios; ex-post ridership against forecast; terrain-specific costs (desert, dunes, sabkha, escarpment); any Gulf outcome data; primary audit documents not reached (Cour des comptes 2014, Korea's BAI, Morocco's audit court on the final Al Boraq cost, the California High-Speed Rail Authority plans). The per-dossier "no reliable data found" lists and the generated gaps table in `README.md` are the full record.
