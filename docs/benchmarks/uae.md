# United Arab Emirates: Etihad Rail (national network; freight operating, passenger service from 30 June 2026; Abu Dhabi–Dubai high-speed line announced)

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026. Every figure below was read on a page opened in this session, except items marked "search summary" or "unverified", which are grade D and are not used in any ratio. Figures are nominal as printed; converted values: see README.md. Forecasts and targets are labelled "announced" and are not outcomes. Quotes were taken with the fetch tool, which returns extracts; the verifier should check wording on the URL given. Several official pages were not readable: WAM article pages returned only a masthead (JavaScript-rendered), and the the AGBI, Arabian Business, Saipem and Groupe SNCF pages returned HTTP 403. WAM is not used as a source for any figure.

Why this system matters for the study. The UAE is the nearest Gulf comparator for a state-owned national rail network, but its public record is short: freight is operating (Stage One completed, per a 2018 statement [^S-bench-ae-043]), the first passenger service started in June 2026 [^S-bench-ae-012], and the first high-speed line is under construction [^S-bench-ae-052]. The record supports description of institutions and announced plans. It does not yet support cost-per-km, ridership-vs-forecast or farebox conclusions.

## 2. Cost per km (nominal and adjusted to SAR 2025; by line, with terrain and tunnel share)

Converted values: see README.md. Nominal values are as printed.

No official cost per km for any UAE line was found in the sources read. The operator's corporate pages carry no capital cost for the freight network, the passenger service or the high-speed line [^S-bench-ae-001] [^S-bench-ae-004] [^S-bench-ae-063]. The following rows are the only per-km figures that can be built from two printed values:

- Stage Two Package A: AED 1,500 m for a 139 km line, awarded in 2019. Computed: 10.79 m AED per km [^S-bench-ae-036] [^S-bench-ae-037]. Price year 2019 is inferred from the article date. This is a contract award value, not an outturn. Scope of the package (civil works, track, signalling) is not stated in the text read. Terrain and tunnel share: not stated.
- Stage Two, all contracts awarded in the phase: US$4.9 bn for a 605 km line (undated article, c.2020). Computed: 8.10 m USD per km [^S-bench-ae-039] [^S-bench-ae-035]. This is contracts awarded, not a completed cost, and the price year is unknown, so it is not converted.

Figures that cannot be used as per-km inputs:

- Other Stage Two package values (Packages B and C at AED 4.4 bn; Package D at AED 4.6 bn; systems at AED 1.6 bn) appear only in search summaries and are grade D, to verify [^S-bench-ae-056] [^S-bench-ae-057] [^S-bench-ae-055].
- Whole-network totals conflict: a search summary gives AED 40 bn (about US$11 bn) for the core network and AED 50 bn for a wider programme [^S-bench-ae-058]; a Zawya/TradeArabia report gives a US$13 bn Etihad Rail plan for a passenger network of 900 km, with scope not stated [^S-bench-ae-054]. These are not divided by length, because scope and price year are not known.
- Hafeet Rail: the figure of US$2.5 bn is the required bank financing, reduced from US$3 bn [^S-bench-ae-045]. It is financing, not capital cost, and it is not used per km. Hafeet's US$1.5 bn of project-finance debt closed in October 2024 [^S-bench-ae-046].
- Stage One: bank financing of US$1.28 bn was secured [^S-bench-ae-032]. Stage One capital cost was not found in any source read.
- Abu Dhabi–Dubai high-speed line: the operator's statement says the cost "is yet to be revealed" [^S-bench-ae-063]. No capital figure was found.

Terrain and tunnel shares: not stated in any source read for any UAE line.

## 3. Ridership vs forecast (forecast at decision, outcome at +5 years; ratio)

No reliable data found for ridership against forecast. The passenger service had run for about four months at 31 Oct 2026. No outturn for a stated period was found that could be set against a forecast for the same period (searched: Etihad Rail corporate pages; Gulf News; The National; Khaleej Times; MEED; 31 Oct 2026). The ratio cannot be computed. Forecast at decision and outturn at +5 years are both absent.

Outturn statements (operator-reported, not audited):

- Around 150,000 people travelled between Abu Dhabi and Fujairah in the first three months, a cumulative count quoted from an Etihad Rail executive [^S-bench-ae-019]. It is not annualised and is not compared with a forecast.
- A search summary reports more than 70,000 tickets sold by early August 2026 (sales, not riders; unverified) [^S-bench-ae-061].

Announced expectations and capacity (no forecast method read; years mostly absent):

- Target of 10 million passengers "when the network is completed", no year [^S-bench-ae-020]; "around 10 million passengers annually" [^S-bench-ae-015]; "expected annual ridership of about 10 million" before launch [^S-bench-ae-013].
- "Up to 21 million passengers annually", Etihad Rail corporate page, undated [^S-bench-ae-006]; the same figure in press in September 2026 [^S-bench-ae-011].
- 36 million passengers a year of network capacity by 2030, per the Minister as reported [^S-bench-ae-028]; analysts project more than 36 million by 2030 [^S-bench-ae-030]; a Gulf Construction report of January 2025 gives "more than 36.5 million" by 2030 for the high-speed-era network [^S-bench-ae-009].

These figures differ by more than a factor of 3.5 between the lowest (10 million [^S-bench-ae-015]) and the highest (36.5 million [^S-bench-ae-009]). They are not forecasts of the same quantity: target, capacity and analyst projection are different things. In the study they are recorded as announced expectations only.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, and the 200–800 km band (air and car)

No reliable data found for population-density or city-size thresholds for UAE rail. No mode-share-by-distance, air–rail substitution or intercity demand study for the UAE was found in one search aimed at peer-reviewed and public-agency sources (searched: 31 Oct 2026). Press coverage gives travel-time and ridership claims, not mode shares.

The 200–800 km band (hypothesis, not asserted) cannot be tested on UAE operating data:

- The announced Abu Dhabi–Dubai high-speed line is about 150 km [^S-bench-ae-051], below the band. Its travel time of about 30 minutes is an announced figure [^S-bench-ae-004].
- Hafeet Rail (UAE–Oman) is 238 km [^S-bench-ae-050], inside the band, but it has no opening date (40% complete in April 2026 [^S-bench-ae-049]; a trial date of Q4 2027 appears only as an unverified headline [^S-bench-ae-060]).
- The Abu Dhabi–Fujairah passenger service is operating, but its route length is not stated in the sources read, so it cannot be placed in the band.
- The national network statement of 900 km [^S-bench-ae-001] is a network total, not a trip length.

Result: the UAE record neither supports nor contradicts the band. A test would need operating ridership by distance pair and mode shares, which do not yet exist in the sources read.

- Passenger network: an Etihad Rail statement of January 2026 gives 11 cities and regions [^S-bench-ae-016]; a Gulf News list of October 2026 gives ten stations once the announced phases are complete [^S-bench-ae-018].
- Operating stations, as of 1 Oct 2026: Mohammed Bin Zayed City (Abu Dhabi), Al Yalayis (Dubai), Fujairah, and Al Dhaid (Sharjah) [^S-bench-ae-017].
- Announced opening dates reported in the same Gulf News list: Madinat Zayed and Liwa on 30 Nov 2026; Al Mirfa, Al Dhannah and Al Sila on 30 Dec 2026; University City (Sharjah) on 30 Mar 2027 [^S-bench-ae-018]. The article does not attribute these dates to an Etihad Rail statement.

- Design speeds: passenger up to 200 km/h [^S-bench-ae-024]; high-speed line up to 350 km/h, per the operator [^S-bench-ae-005] and press [^S-bench-ae-007]. A 320 km/h figure in an unverified summary [^S-bench-ae-062] conflicts.

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Cost overrun: no reliable data found. A cost overrun needs a printed decision-stage estimate and a printed outturn for the same line. Neither was found for any UAE line. No observation is written to overruns-uae.csv (header only).

Schedule evidence (dated statements, not measured overruns):

- Stage Two tendering was suspended on 26 Jan 2016. The Chairman said "a decision has been taken to suspend the tendering process for Stage Two" [^S-bench-ae-044].
- In November 2018 the Minister said 2018 "was unfortunately not a possible deadline" and that the UAE "would like to see a complete rail network by 2021" [^S-bench-ae-041]. The sources read show the 2021 target was not met: the operator described passenger services as phased from 2026 [^S-bench-ae-003], and Stage Two construction began in January 2020 [^S-bench-ae-040]. The original Stage Two opening date was not found, so the slip in years cannot be computed.
- Stage Two financing was agreed in November 2018 by the federal Ministry of Finance and the Abu Dhabi Department of Finance; the amount was not disclosed [^S-bench-ae-033].
- High-speed line: the contractor's completion date is 2031 [^S-bench-ae-053]. An earlier date in an unverified search summary is not used. A 2032 date on the RTA Dubai network map [^S-bench-ae-064] is a map date, not a completion date.
- Hafeet Rail: no official opening date. The progress announcement is 40% complete [^S-bench-ae-049].

Renegotiations and scope changes visible in the record: the Hafeet bank financing requirement fell from US$3 bn to US$2.5 bn [^S-bench-ae-045]. The full-network length moved from 1,200 km in 2018–2019 [^S-bench-ae-034] [^S-bench-ae-038] to 900 km on the operator's current site [^S-bench-ae-001]. No explanation of the change was found in the sources read; the change is escalated, not interpreted.

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

No reliable data found. No station-level ridership, no before-and-after study and no intermediate-station study for UAE rail were found (searched: Etihad Rail corporate pages; Gulf News; The National; Khaleej Times; 31 Oct 2026).

The network does plan stops in small places, and these are the relevant test cases: Al Dhaid (Sharjah) is open [^S-bench-ae-017]; Madinat Zayed, Liwa, Al Mirfa, Al Dhannah and Al Sila are announced for late 2026 [^S-bench-ae-018]. No ridership, catchment or employment result for any of these stops was found, so the direction of effect is not known. Evidence is absent, not mixed or negative.

Literature from Spain, Japan and France is covered in those dossiers and is not repeated here.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- Institutional form. The UAE network is owned by the Abu Dhabi government (70%) and the federal government (30%) as reported in 2018 [^S-bench-ae-031], and is run by a developer-operator that built freight first. Passenger service was added in 2026 through a 50:50 operating JV with Keolis [^S-bench-ae-026]. Evidence from this one case shows the sequence (freight first, then passenger service through an operator JV). It does not show whether that sequence earned a return.
- Forecasts are not outcomes. The passenger projections range from 10 to 36.5 million a year [^S-bench-ae-015] [^S-bench-ae-006] [^S-bench-ae-009], with different bases. Evidence suggests that projection-stage figures should be recorded with their basis. The early operating figure (about 150,000 in three months [^S-bench-ae-019]) is cumulative and operator-reported, so it cannot yet be used to judge any forecast.
- Financing structure. Stage One was bank-financed (US$1.28 bn) [^S-bench-ae-032]; Hafeet used project-finance debt [^S-bench-ae-046]. Financing amounts are not capital costs, and the UAE record does not give a per-km cost that can be set against other systems.
- Schedule. The Stage Two suspension in 2016 and the 2021 target that was not met show how schedules were reset [^S-bench-ae-044] [^S-bench-ae-041]. The record has no measured cost outturn.
- Distance band. The UAE's only open-line and under-construction examples are short (about 150 km high-speed line [^S-bench-ae-051]) or freight-led (900 km network [^S-bench-ae-001]). The 200–800 km band is therefore not tested by UAE data.
