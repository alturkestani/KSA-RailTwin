# Morocco: Al Boraq (Tangier–Kenitra high-speed line, Casablanca corridor) and the Kenitra–Marrakech extension

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026 by the R1b collector. Every figure below was read on the page or PDF named in its footnote, except where marked "unverified" (search summary or page not opened, HTTP 403) or "secondary" (Wikipedia). The only audit-court document reached is the Cour des comptes report on ONCF (published as PDF, text covers FY2015, before the line opened). ONCF's own website (oncf.ma) refused the connection on 31 Oct 2026, the Arab Fund project pages returned HTTP 403, and the Medias24 and Railway Journal pages were not readable, so most operator figures are reported by Moroccan press that cites an ONCF communiqué (grade C). Figures are nominal, in the currency of the source. Conversion to SAR 2025 is generated in `README.md` (converted values: see README.md). Metric rows are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-morocco.csv` and overrun candidates in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-morocco.csv`. Escalations are listed at the end.

Two names matter. "Al Boraq" is the high-speed service on the Tangier–Kenitra line, which is connected by conventional track to Rabat and Casablanca; the press gives the Tangier–Casablanca corridor between 323 and 363 km (Section 1). The Kenitra–Marrakech line is announced and under contract, not built; its figures are planned.

## 3. Ridership vs forecast (forecast at decision, outcome at +5 years; ratio)

Forecast at opening. ONCF told AFP in Nov 2018 that it expected six million passengers "après trois ans d'exploitation" [^S-bench-ma-012]. The Railway Gazette in Nov 2019 reported an ONCF expectation of 3 million passengers in the first full calendar year [^S-bench-ma-014]. No earlier forecast (the feasibility or investment-decision study) was found; the 2018 forecast is the earliest found.

Reading the table. The year-3 forecast of six million was reached at 43% in 2021 [^S-bench-ma-020][^S-bench-ma-012]. Outturns rose in every year reported (2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025; 2023 not verified in an opened source). The level in 2020–2021 is not explained in the sources read. A November 2025 press item had described the 5.6 million figure as projected [^S-bench-ma-027]; the April 2026 board communiqué gives it as the year's total [^S-bench-ma-026]. The ratio is a press-reported ONCF figure against a press-reported ONCF forecast, not an audited comparison (grade C). A 2019 full-year figure of 3 million appears only in a search summary (Medias24 page not readable) and is not used [^S-bench-ma-042].

Tangier airport (all routes, not route-specific). The Tangier Ibn Battouta airport handled 1,913,698 passengers in 2023, up 34%, per ONDA as reported by MAP [^S-bench-ma-028]. No route-level air count for Tangier–Casablanca was found (Section 5).

## 4. Farebox recovery / operating ratio (definition used by the source)

No reliable data found for an Al Boraq operating ratio or farebox recovery. Searched: ONCF communiqués as reported in Moroccan press (2019–2026), the Cour des comptes ONCF report; oncf.ma refused the connection (31 Oct 2026); searched 31 Oct 2026. The published Al Boraq figures are revenue only: 165 MDH in 2020, 344 MDH in 2021 [^S-bench-ma-020][^S-bench-ma-021]; 562 MDH in 2022 [^S-bench-ma-023]; 780 MDH in 2024 [^S-bench-ma-025]; 848 MDH in 2025 [^S-bench-ma-026]. None of these is set against operating cost.

The only statement on cost cover is ONCF's own claim, that the operating margin ensured "all operating costs were covered" (Nov 2019) [^S-bench-ma-015]. No cost or margin figure accompanied it. It is recorded as a claim, not a ratio.

Network context (ONCF, not Al Boraq). The Cour des comptes reports that ONCF's social net result was −279 MDH in 2014 and +16 MDH in 2015 [^S-bench-ma-004]. It also notes that the consolidated result was worse than the social one because of subsidiaries. This is network-wide and pre-opening for Al Boraq; it is not a farebox ratio.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, and the 200–800 km band (test against air and car)

Density and city-size thresholds: no reliable data found (searched: Cour des comptes ONCF report, ONCF statements, the 2024 Hoummirat and Blayac abstract summary, and the CUNY thesis abstract; 31 Oct 2026). No Moroccan figure for a minimum population or density for the line was found.

The 200–800 km band (a hypothesis, not asserted). The Tangier–Casablanca corridor is about 323–363 km by the press figures [^S-bench-ma-035][^S-bench-ma-016], so it sits inside the band by length. Testing the band needs mode-share or air–rail substitution data for this corridor. What was found:
- Air: the only airport figure is the national-airport total for Tangier (1.91 million in 2023, all routes) [^S-bench-ma-028]. No Tangier–Casablanca air count was found: no reliable data found (searched: ONDA statistics pages; the ONDA 2019 financial report PDF link returned an HTML error page, 31 Oct 2026).
- Car: a 2026 master's thesis (CUNY, geography) reports, in its abstract, "mode shift from automobiles" after Al Boraq opened, based on ONCF ridership data and 234 rider surveys [^S-bench-ma-040]. The thesis is not peer-reviewed; the full text was not read; no mode-share figure is in the abstract.
- Time savings: a 2024 pre-launch discrete-choice study of travel-time value across Tangier, Kenitra, Rabat, Casablanca and Marrakesh (Hoummirat and Blayac, Applied Economics) was seen only in a search summary; its abstract warns that pricing could counterbalance time savings [^S-bench-ma-041]. Unverified; not used for any figure.
- Planned travel time at opening: about two hours Casablanca–Tangier, "around two hours" (press, 2018, planned) [^S-bench-ma-008]. The same press item also says the 200 km journey is halved, which conflicts with the corridor length (Escalation E5).

Verdict on the band for Morocco: not tested. No reliable, opened source gives mode shares by distance or air–rail substitution for the corridor. The evidence found points to mode shift from cars (thesis abstract, grade C) and to time-saving value (abstract, unverified), with no measured air effect.

## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

Stopping pattern (main stops). Secondary sources list Tanger-Ville, Kenitra, Rabat-Agdal and Casa-Voyageurs as the principal stops on the Tangier–Casablanca service [^S-bench-ma-037]. Opening press also described the Casablanca–Tangier time as "around two hours" [^S-bench-ma-008]. Timetable frequency and the number of daily trains vary by season; the figures found in search summaries are unverified and not used. A timetable from oncf.ma is needed.

Stations and spacing. The press lists the Kenitra–Marrakech planned stops (Kenitra, Rabat, a stadium stop at Benslimane, Nouaceur airport, Skhour Rhamna, Benguerir, Marrakech Palmeraie terminus) as unattributed press [^S-bench-ma-030][^S-bench-ma-029]. No reliable data found for station spacing (km between stations) on either line; the Tangier-Kenitra stop-by-stop distances were not found.

Test speed (not a service speed): a 357 km/h test was reported in 2018 [^S-bench-ma-006]; the design speed is 320 km/h [^S-bench-ma-016]. The Kenitra–Casablanca section was rated for 160 km/h at opening and planned for 220 km/h (Wikipedia, secondary, not used) [^S-bench-ma-035].

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Cost. The best-supported candidate is the Cour des comptes 20 MdDH envelope for this line in the 2010–2015 contract programme [^S-bench-ma-001] against the completed 22.9 bn MAD press figure [^S-bench-ma-006]: a computed ratio of 1.145 (+14.5%) on nominal figures [^S-bench-ma-001][^S-bench-ma-006] [overrun row OV-ma-001]. The same Cour report prints 22.9 bn for the same line as the 2010–2015 plan figure [^S-bench-ma-002], which would give a ratio of 1.00 if that is the outturn [^S-bench-ma-002] (OV-ma-002). An AFP dispatch reports the cost as "environ 15% de plus que les estimations initiales" [^S-bench-ma-009], unattributed and unquantified against a baseline (grade D; OV-ma-003). The sources do not establish which reading is right, and no renegotiation was found.

Cause given by the Cour des comptes for slow delivery to 2015: "la procédure d’expropriation et les difficultés techniques (tremblements de terre, le vent et la pluie)" [^S-bench-ma-003]. The cumulative 2015 realisation was 18 MdDH against the plan (79%) [^S-bench-ma-002]; this is not a final cost.

Schedule. The ADFD statement in Nov 2016 targeted a connection "by 2018" [^S-bench-ma-018]; the completed line is reported as opened in Nov 2018 [^S-bench-ma-006]: zero years against that target [^S-bench-ma-018][^S-bench-ma-006] (OV-ma-005). Against the 2010–2015 plan window in which the Cour table places the line, the difference is three years [^S-bench-ma-002] (inferred baseline; OV-ma-004). A search summary of the Railway Journal says the opening was delayed by land acquisition and subsidence; that page was not readable [^S-bench-ma-043] and is not used as a finding.

Ridership against forecast is set out in Section 3 (ratios 0.43, 0.70, 0.92, 0.93 [^S-bench-ma-020][^S-bench-ma-022][^S-bench-ma-024][^S-bench-ma-026]; OV-ma-006 to OV-ma-009). These are candidate observations, not uplifts.

Kenitra–Marrakech: no overrun can be measured. The opening is planned for Sep–Nov 2029 (RG 2024) [^S-bench-ma-031] or "d'ici 2030" (Hespress 2025) [^S-bench-ma-033]: a date conflict in the sources, not an overrun.

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

No empirical study of the effect of Al Boraq on intermediate places (Kenitra, Rabat-Agdal, or smaller towns on the corridor) was found. No reliable data found (searched: CUNY Academic Works, HAL/INRAE, Applied Economics, ONCF and press; 31 Oct 2026).

What was found:
- CUNY master's thesis (2026), ex-post, Geography: ONCF ridership data, 234 rider surveys, nine months of fieldwork. The abstract reports "accelerated passenger growth, substantial induced demand, and mode shift from automobiles" [^S-bench-ma-040]. It does not report effects on the intermediate stations; the full text was not read. Grade C (student thesis; abstract only).
- Hoummirat and Blayac (2024, Applied Economics), a pre-launch discrete-choice study covering Tangier, Kenitra, Rabat, Casablanca and Marrakesh; its value-of-time result is about travellers, not about local economies [^S-bench-ma-041]. Unverified (access blocked; search summary only).
- Press claims that the line "stimulates economic growth in those regions" are projections, not measured outcomes, and are not used.

Direction of effect for intermediate stations: not measured. Evidence is absent, not negative or positive. This dossier does not infer one.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- The line was built and financed as a state-owned operator project, with a French loan share and Gulf development-fund co-financing [^S-bench-ma-005][^S-bench-ma-007]. The financing detail is press-reported; the fund primaries were not read.
- Ridership rose in every reported year from 2020 to 2025 (1.3 to 5.6 million passengers; 2023 not verified) [^S-bench-ma-020][^S-bench-ma-026]. Against the opening forecast of six million [^S-bench-ma-012], the ratio was 0.43 at year 3 and 0.93 at year 7 (press-reported ONCF figures; grade C) [^S-bench-ma-020][^S-bench-ma-026].
- The completed cost is reported as 22.9 bn MAD [^S-bench-ma-006], equal to the plan figure in the Cour des comptes report; the overrun cannot be settled from the sources read (Escalation E1). Press reports critics who questioned spending on the line relative to education and health [^S-bench-ma-006].
- No operating-cost, farebox or operating-ratio data for the line is public in the sources read; ONCF states that operating costs were covered (2019) [^S-bench-ma-015]. Any KSA comparison on cost recovery would need the ONCF accounts.
- The distance band (323–363 km corridor) is not tested by Moroccan evidence; no air–rail figure was found (Section 5).
- The Kenitra–Marrakech extension is announced and contracted in part (rolling stock, 2025), with dates that differ across sources; there is no outturn to learn from yet.

## Escalations

- E1 (Cost, judgement needed). The 22.9 bn MAD completed-cost press figure [^S-bench-ma-006] equals the 2010–2015 plan figure for the same line in the Cour des comptes report [^S-bench-ma-002]. Either the press figure is the budget, or the plan figure happened to match. Needed: ONCF final accounts or the Cour des comptes review after 2015. Both readings are kept as overrun rows OV-ma-001 and OV-ma-002.
- E2 (Cost, internal conflict in the same report). The same Cour report prints 20 MdDH for the line in the envelope [^S-bench-ma-001] and 22.9 in the plan table [^S-bench-ma-002]. Both are kept.
- E3 (Overrun, unattributed). AFP's "environ 15% de plus que les estimations initiales" [^S-bench-ma-009] has no named source or baseline. Grade D; not used in totals.
- E4 (Cost, scale). The ADFD release says "AED515 million" for "the entirety" of the project [^S-bench-ma-019]. This cannot be the total at the MAD figures in the sources, and the scope is not explained. Not used for per-km.
- E5 (Length, conflicts). The Tangier–Kenitra length is 180 km (AFP, D) [^S-bench-ma-011], 186 km (Wikipedia, D) [^S-bench-ma-035], 200 km (ADFD, A) [^S-bench-ma-018] and 200 km (Railway Gazette 2024, D) [^S-bench-ma-029]. The Tangier–Casablanca corridor is 323 km (Wikipedia lead, D), 350 km (Wikipedia infobox, D) [^S-bench-ma-035], 363 km (Railway Gazette 2019, C) [^S-bench-ma-016]. Reuters/AP (2018, C) also prints "the 200 km (124 miles) Casablanca-Tangier journey" [^S-bench-ma-008]. Per-km ranges are shown as ranges for this reason.
- E6 (Financing, conflicts). Press reports of the Saudi Fund contribution differ in currency, in whether it is a grant or a loan, and in amount; the Arab Fund pages give KD 55.7 million in a search summary (unverified) [^S-bench-ma-038]. The French AFD share is reported only in unverified search summaries, with no figure used here. A Kuwait loan of 450 million dirhams is a search snippet (unverified) [^S-bench-ma-039]. Not resolved; primary fund records needed (SFD, KFAED, AFD, ADFD site). Not resolved; primary fund records needed (SFD, KFAED, AFD, ADFD site).
- E7 (Ridership, 2019). The full-year 2019 figure of 3 million appears in a Medias24 headline only (page not readable) [^S-bench-ma-042]. Not used. The 2020–2021 level (1.3 and 2.59 million) is not explained in the sources read.
- E8 (Stops). Wikipedia names Rabat-Agdal; another search summary names Rabat-Ville [^S-bench-ma-037]. The current Rabat station is not confirmed in an opened source; no stop-level timetable was read.
- E9 (Kenitra–Marrakech dates and cost). 2029 (Sep–Nov) [^S-bench-ma-031] against "d'ici 2030" [^S-bench-ma-033]; cost 40 bn MAD without a year, unattributed [^S-bench-ma-030]. The 781 M EUR contract covers rolling stock, not civil works [^S-bench-ma-032]. The 2025 Kenitra–Settat signalling contract (260 M EUR) is preparatory [^S-bench-ma-034].
- E10 (Operating data). ONCF's claim that operating costs are covered (2019) [^S-bench-ma-015] has no supporting figure. An operating ratio cannot be built from the sources read. Needed: ONCF annual reports and accounts (oncf.ma; connection refused on 31 Oct 2026).
- E11 (Audit). The Cour des comptes annual report 2024–2025 (Bulletin officiel, 23 Jan 2026) was seen in a search summary only; its ONCF content was not read. Needed for any post-opening audit finding.
- E12 (Small-town evidence). The two empirical items (CUNY thesis abstract; Hoummirat and Blayac abstract) do not measure intermediate-station effects. The Hoummirat and Blayac abstract is unverified. No peer-reviewed post-opening evaluation was found.
- E13 (Licence). Press pages and Wikipedia are used only as secondary or with grade C/D flags; the Wikipedia page is CC BY-SA on its own terms (not checked in this session). The ONCF Cour des comptes PDF is a Moroccan public document; its reuse terms were not checked.
