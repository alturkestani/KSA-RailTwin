# Turkey: YHT high-speed trains (TCDD Taşımacılık; TCDD infrastructure; Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure)

Scope: the Turkish YHT (yüksek hızlı tren) network. Operator TCDD Taşımacılık AŞ; infrastructure TCDD; the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure contracts public-service-obligation (KHY) payments. Converted values: see README.md. Money is shown nominal, in the currency and price year the source prints. Evidence is thin on the ex-post side: one primary audit (Sayıştay, 2018 accounts) carries most of the operating figures. Most cost figures for the newer lines come from press citing opposition MPs, and are graded D.

## 3. Ridership vs forecast

Ex-ante ridership forecasts for the YHT lines were not found in any document read. The only forecast-versus-outturn figures read are contract forecasts of ticket revenue in the Ministry's public-service contracts, which the Sayıştay audit compares with accounting revenue [^S-bench-tr-007]. They are revenue ratios, not passenger ratios, and are not in the ridership metric.

| line or contract | forecast (2017 contract) | accounting outturn 2017 | deviation (printed) | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YHT ticket revenue, contract forecast vs accounting | TRY 242,000,000 | TRY 188,680,609 | −22.03 % | [^S-bench-tr-007] |
| YHT ticket revenue, 2018 contract | TRY 234,958,336 | TRY 238,179,613 | +1.37 % | [^S-bench-tr-007] |

Caveat stated by the audit: the first contract ran 16 months for 2017 and the second 8 months for 2018 [^S-bench-tr-005]. Passengers did rise: 7,163,102 in 2017 to 8,104,363 in 2018, +13.14 % [^S-bench-tr-005]. The Ministry's response in the audit says the forecast was set by revaluing the previous year's actual revenue for inflation; the audit says the printed figures do not confirm this, and it sees no economic basis for indexing a revenue line to inflation [^S-bench-tr-001].

## 4. Farebox recovery and operating ratio

Definitions as printed by the Sayıştay (2018 accounts, two contract years): the farebox ratio is net ticket revenue divided by the service production cost ("hizmet üretim giderlerini karşılama oranı") [^S-bench-tr-003]. The operating ratio here is computed as total cost divided by total accrued revenue including the PSO [^S-bench-tr-002].

| measure | 2017 | 2018 | grade | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Farebox (ticket revenue / cost, excluding PSO), printed | 79 % | 73 % | A | [^S-bench-tr-003] |
| Net ticket revenue, TRY | 188,680,608.96 | 238,179,613.29 | A | [^S-bench-tr-002] |
| Total production cost, TRY | 239,305,207.21 | 324,878,457.46 | A | [^S-bench-tr-002] |
| PSO accrued, TRY | 103,741,471.06 | 133,168,938.03 | A | [^S-bench-tr-002] |
| Operating ratio incl. PSO (computed: cost / total accrued revenue) | 0.8184 | 0.8749 | A | [^S-bench-tr-002] |
| Loss without PSO (cost minus ticket revenue), TRY, printed as 'Zarar' | 50,624,598.25 | 86,698,844 | A | [^S-bench-tr-006] |

The audit concludes that with the PSO included the operator earned on average about 18 % above its costs over the two contract years [^S-bench-tr-004], and that profit above 10 % with PSO is not consistent with a PSO for commercially viable lines [^S-bench-tr-008]. The Ministry response in the audit says half of any surplus over cost is returned to the PSO operator as an incentive, and that ticket revenue forms in a free market; the audit answers that the maximum fares are set in the contract and changed only with Ministry approval [^S-bench-tr-001]. These two readings conflict and are escalated (section Escalations).

Post-2018 operating results: a press report of the Sayıştay 2023 audit gives TCDD Taşımacılık AŞ a 2023 period loss of TRY 2.8 bn; it does not give a YHT figure and the audit document was not located [^S-bench-tr-014]. No YHT-specific operating ratio after 2018 found in a primary source.

## 5. Thresholds: density, city size, and the 200–800 km band

Density threshold: no reliable data found (searched: Sayıştay 2018, EIB project pages, UIC; Turkish queries on density and HSR thresholds, 31 Oct 2026). Not tested.

City-size threshold: no reliable data found (searched as above; no city-size threshold appears in any source read). Not tested.

The 200–800 km band, tested for Turkey (hypothesis, not asserted):

- Evidence for rail competitiveness on the corridor: the Sayıştay audit reports occupancy of 84–92 % on the Ankara–İstanbul YHT in 2017–2018 and rising passenger counts on all four lines [^S-bench-tr-001] [^S-bench-tr-005]. The corridor length is 490 km in the EIB description [^S-bench-tr-010], which sits inside the band.
- Air-to-rail evidence: a 2017 user survey at four stations (peer-reviewed proceedings; abstract only read) reports a modal shift "mainly from road transportation" on the short Ankara–Eskişehir corridor, and that "shift from air to HSR was also observed" on Ankara–İstanbul [^S-bench-tr-015]. No share is printed in the abstract; survey size not read. No DHMI air-passenger statistics were read in this session (not yet searched; next step).
- Accessibility evidence: a peer-reviewed-status-unconfirmed 2020 paper on the Konya–Ankara–İstanbul link reports that HSR improves intercity accessibility and shifts travel demand toward rail; no figure was readable [^S-bench-tr-016].
- Against a commercial reading inside the band: ticket revenue covered 73–79 % of production cost in 2017–2018 for the YHT lines together [^S-bench-tr-003]. The gap is covered by PSO payments in the contract [^S-bench-tr-002]. This does not show the band is wrong; it shows that the Turkish lines were not commercially self-financing on ticket revenue in those two years.
- Ankara–Konya (about 309 km total, UIC snippet, unverified [^S-bench-tr-018]) lies in the band; no air-share effect for it was found.

Net: the Turkish evidence is consistent with the band as a range where rail carries large volumes, but no study found tests distance-band mode shares with a published share and a counterfactual. The band remains a hypothesis here.

Gaps: "rail_share_by_distance" for Turkey: no reliable data found (searched: Turkish and English queries on air–rail and modal share, 31 Oct 2026; DHMI statistics not yet searched).

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Schedule, Ankara–Sivas (the clearest overrun in the sources):
- First announced opening 2012, per a press report [^S-bench-tr-013]; the opening was postponed seven times, per the same report (other press counts vary; not verified). Actual opening 26 April 2023 [^S-bench-tr-012]. About 11 years from the first announced date (computed, grade D because the first date is press-only).

Capex:
- Istanbul–Ankara EIB appraisal estimates: EUR 2.5 bn (2006) to EUR 3.5 bn (2011), +40 % (computed). This is estimate-to-estimate, not an outturn; scope comparison not confirmed [^S-bench-tr-011] [^S-bench-tr-009].
- Ankara–Sivas: programme figures TRY 24,946 m (2022) to TRY 64,529 m (2023), +159 % (computed), press-cited from a CHP deputy; not an outturn; grade D [^S-bench-tr-013].
- No primary outturn cost for any YHT line was found.

Ridership and revenue: see section 3 (revenue against contract forecast, −22.03 % in 2017 and +1.37 % in 2018) [^S-bench-tr-007].

Renegotiations: the Sayıştay audit finds that the Ministry's PSO contracts set maximum fares and that fare rises were not made for two years; it asks for a re-analysis of whether the YHT can run without PSO [^S-bench-tr-001]. No renegotiation of a YHT contract with a cost figure was found.

Non-YHT context (not in the overrun file): the same 2018 audit reports delays on an IPA-funded conventional modernisation project, with EU funds lost because the works ran late [^S-bench-tr-001]. It is not a YHT line and is excluded from the overrun file.

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

Result: no empirical study of intermediate stations on the Turkish YHT (Eskişehir, Bilecik, Bozüyük, Polatlı) was found. Searches on 31 Oct 2026 covered English and Turkish queries on station effects, regional development and station-area impact [^S-bench-tr-025]. The evidence is therefore absent, not mixed.

What was found, graded:
- A Turkish-language study of Konya HSR (repository record, peer-review status unknown, snippet only) reports that YHT effects "tended to be concentrated in the city centre" of the region and then spread through other modes [^S-bench-tr-021]. Grade C at most, not read in full. It concerns a city, not a small town; no figure.
- A METU study on HSR station-area development in Ankara is a city-centre study and was not read [^S-bench-tr-025] (search summary).
- The Baş and Yüzer 2020 paper (Konya–Ankara–İstanbul) is about inter-city accessibility, not intermediate towns [^S-bench-tr-016].

Direction: mixed or unknown for intermediate stations. The only qualitative hint (Konya) points to city-centre concentration, which is negative for towns outside the centre, but it is not a test of small towns and is unverified.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- The Turkish operating record is one audit year pair (2017–2018) with PSO payments; it shows that YHT ticket revenue alone covered 73–79 % of cost in those years [^S-bench-tr-003], and that the PSO design was itself questioned by the auditor [^S-bench-tr-008].
- Contract fares set by the state, with fare rises deferred for two years, were the auditor's example of a revenue-side risk [^S-bench-tr-001].
- Estimate growth on the EIB appraisal (EUR 2.5 bn to EUR 3.5 bn) and the press-cited programme growth on Ankara–Sivas show that early cost figures moved materially; neither is an outturn [^S-bench-tr-011] [^S-bench-tr-009] [^S-bench-tr-013].
- Schedule: the Ankara–Sivas opening moved from a first announced 2012 date to 2023 per press [^S-bench-tr-013] [^S-bench-tr-012].
- The evidence for distance-band mode share is corridor-level and survey-based [^S-bench-tr-015]; a test for KSA would need a counterfactual for air and car, which none of the Turkish sources provide.

## Escalations

1. Conflicting Ankara–Sivas cost figures. Press citing CHP Deputy Chairman Ahmet Akın gives TRY 24,946 m (2022 programme) and TRY 64,529 m (2023 programme) [^S-bench-tr-013]; other press totals in search summaries differ and were not opened. The SBB Yatırım Programı documents were not retrieved. Both figures kept at grade D; they need the official programme documents before any use.
2. Ticket pricing: the Sayıştay audit says fares are set in the PSO contract and changed only with Ministry approval; the Ministry response in the audit says fares are market-set [^S-bench-tr-001]. Conflict recorded, not resolved.
3. Corridor length: the EIB description gives 490 km [^S-bench-tr-010]. Press search summaries gave a different corridor length (not captured with a URL, not used). No second primary length was read; the 490 km figure is kept at grade A.
4. The Ankara–Istanbul EIB estimate changed from EUR 2.5 bn (2006) to EUR 3.5 bn (2011); the scope is not confirmed as identical [^S-bench-tr-011] [^S-bench-tr-009]. Estimate-to-estimate; a cross-check against the line's audited cost is needed.
5. The only primary YHT operating data is 2017–2018 (one audit). A decision needs a later audit or the TCDD Taşımacılık annual report. Escalate to coordinator; the Sayıştay 2023 TCDD audit document (not located) is the next target.
6. Two official sources could not be read on 31 Oct 2026: the Ministry KHY 2024 report (connection refused) [^S-bench-tr-022] and the TCDD 2023 sector report (404) [^S-bench-tr-023]. A 2024 rail passenger headline (279.4 million) [^S-bench-tr-024] is not used until a primary is read.
