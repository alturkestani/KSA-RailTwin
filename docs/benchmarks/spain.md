# Spain: AVE (ADIF Alta Velocidad; Renfe; IRYO and OUIGO as open-access operators)

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026. Figures below were read in this session on the page or PDF named in their footnote, except where marked "secondary" (press, or a summary of a primary report not opened) or "unverified" (search snippet or not opened). The verification list in Escalations names every source re-opened and every exception. Figures are in the currency and price basis of each source. Spain's ECA figures are nominal, as of the audit, with the price year inferred as 2018 (the report year). Conversion to SAR 2025 is generated in `README.md` with the single price index `cities/ksa/costs/index.yaml` (CP2 P2a, A-cost-002; it replaced `index-r1b.yaml` and A-bench-001). Metric rows are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-spain.csv` (nominal values as printed) and overrun candidates in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-spain.csv`. Evidence is mostly grade A (European Court of Auditors, the Cortes Generales resolutions, the national rail regulator CNMC). The AIReF figures are secondary (reproduced by ADBI); the press figures are grade D. Escalations are listed at the end.

## 3. Ridership against forecast

No Spanish line-level ridership-versus-forecast ratio at +5 years was found in the primary sources read.

- Madrid-Seville. The officially projected five million annual trips "was not to be reached until more than a decade later (by 2005)"[^S-bench-es-006]. The outturn volume is not printed, so no ratio is computed.
- Madrid-Valencia. Press reporting of AIReF gives real 2015 demand of 2.1 million and the lowest potential demand in the studies as below 3.4 million, a ratio of 0.62 computed from press (grade D; indicative only)[^S-bench-es-008]. The same press report says the studies overstated passengers by "entre un 38% y un 52%"[^S-bench-es-008] (grade D).
- ECA benchmark. ECA says a high-speed line "should ideally have nine million passengers per year to be successful" (summary point IX), or at least six million in the opening year (para 79), and sources the figure to academic and institutional literature[^S-bench-es-001]. In 2016 only three audited lines exceeded nine million: Madrid-Barcelona, Turin-Salerno and LGV Est-Europeenne[^S-bench-es-001]. Eje Atlantico, Rhin-Rhone and Madrid-Leon carried "far lower" numbers[^S-bench-es-001]. This is an audit benchmark, not a Spanish planning test.
- Madrid-Barcelona demand growth. The rail share on this corridor rose from 63.3 % (2019) to 81.5 % (2024) on the CNMC count[^S-bench-es-005]; section 5 gives the detail. Madrid-Barcelona is one of the three audited lines the ECA reports above nine million in 2016[^S-bench-es-001].
- Network totals. HSR carried 31.63 million passengers in 2023[^S-bench-es-004] and 39.6 million in 2024, up 22.3 %[^S-bench-es-005]. These are network totals, not forecast comparisons.

## 5. Thresholds: density, city size, and the 200-800 km band

Density and city size. No reliable data found for a population-density or city-size threshold for Spanish HSR (searched: ECA 2018, ADBI 2026, CNMC 2024 and 2025; 2026-10-31). ADBI describes the plateau the network was built on as "flat and also sparsely populated" as a qualitative cost explanation[^S-bench-es-006], with no threshold. ECA's catchment analysis counts people within 15, 30 and 60 minutes of each station (Annex VIII; station-level data not extracted in this pass). The ECA's catchment verdict for individual stations is in section 8.

The 200-800 km band (hypothesis; tested, not assumed). Evidence for and against, from Spain:
- For high rail shares on corridors of about 365 to 621 km with frequent HSR:
  - Madrid-Barcelona, rail share 81.5 % in 2024 and 81.8 % in 2023 (CNMC), with a 621 km non-stop service[^S-bench-es-005][^S-bench-es-001].
  - Madrid-Valencia, rail share 93.3 % in 2024 (line 365 km per ADBI)[^S-bench-es-005][^S-bench-es-006].
  - Madrid-Seville, rail share 90.4 % in 2024 (line 476 km per ADBI)[^S-bench-es-005][^S-bench-es-006].
  - ECA: on Madrid-Barcelona, HSR is "the fastest travel option" door to door, and "the modal split between air and rail has changed from 85:15 to 38:62 in 2016"[^S-bench-es-001] (order air:rail read from context; Escalation E-2).
- Against, or limits:
  - The regulator says rail's share "baja del 50%" for trips longer than 180 minutes, even on full HSR, citing Barcelona-Sevilla and Barcelona-Malaga[^S-bench-es-004]. The distances of those two routes are not stated in the text read, so the 180-minute threshold cannot be converted into km here.
  - Madrid-Santiago de Compostela, 41.9 % (2024), and Madrid-Vigo, 31.9 % (2024)[^S-bench-es-005]. ECA names Madrid-Santiago and Madrid-Leon among audited lines with "very few connections"[^S-bench-es-001]; the CNMC text does not attribute these shares to a cause.
  - Spain-France routes with both air and rail: rail share 12 %, which the regulator links to few frequencies and high prices[^S-bench-es-005].
  - ECA: the Madrid-Barcelona direct train takes 150 minutes against 190 with stops; ECA says the situation on the Madrid-Santiago line is similar to routes that are "not practicable by conventional rail"[^S-bench-es-001].

Read-out. Spanish evidence supports high rail shares on the 365 to 621 km corridors where frequency and door-to-door time are competitive, and shows rail share falling beyond 180 minutes. It does not test the 800 km upper bound (no Spanish route above 800 km is measured in these sources), and it does not isolate distance from frequency, price and connections. Car: no reliable car mode-share data found in the sources read (searched: CNMC 2024 and 2025, ECA 2018; 2026-10-31). The ECA door-to-door table is not used because its text layout is ambiguous for the car row.

## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

- Station spacing. ECA counts four to 15 stations per audited line and distances between stations on the same line "the longest distance ... is 253 km; the shortest is 26 km"[^S-bench-es-001] (across all audited lines, not Spain-only).
- Stop penalty. "each intermediate stop extends total travel time by an average of four to 12 minutes, and reduces average speed by three to 16 km/h" (ECA, timetable data, all audited lines)[^S-bench-es-001].
- Madrid-Barcelona pattern. A non-stop service covers 621 km; other trains also serve Guadalajara, Calatayud, Zaragoza, Lleida and Camp de Tarragona[^S-bench-es-001]. The direct train takes 150 minutes and the stopping train 190 minutes[^S-bench-es-001]; average speeds are 268 km/h (direct) and 211 km/h (stopping)[^S-bench-es-001].
- Frequencies. The CNMC describes "escasas frecuencias" on international services[^S-bench-es-005]; frequency tables for domestic services were not extracted.

## 7. Overruns (cost and schedule) and renegotiations

Cost, audited lines (ECA Table 5; nominal; "actual cost overrun" as printed):
- Madrid-Barcelona-French border: 38.5 %; initial estimate 8,740 m EUR, total 12,109 m EUR[^S-bench-es-001].
- Madrid-Leon: 33.3 %; 4,062 m EUR to 5,415 m EUR[^S-bench-es-001].
- Eje Atlantico: 26.3 %; 2,055 m EUR to 2,596 m EUR[^S-bench-es-001].
- ECA: "all audited high-speed lines had cost overruns of more than 25 %"[^S-bench-es-001]. Madrid-Galicia has no line-level estimate (ECA marks n/a).

Cost, Madrid-Barcelona contracts, Tribunal de Cuentas (audit of main contracts 2002 to opening; reported by the Cortes Generales in 2014): the final cost "ha sido un 43,2 % superior del precio de adjudicación de los contratos originales", and the average deviation against the tender budget was 31.4 %[^S-bench-es-003]. These two bases are different and are kept as separate rows.

Schedule. 336 extensions averaged "un incremento medio de 13,5 meses" in execution periods for the audited contracts[^S-bench-es-003]. ECA Table 2 gives years from planning to opening: Madrid-Barcelona 25 years (1988 to 2013), Madrid-Leon 17 (1998 to 2015), Eje Atlantico 17 (1998 to 2015), Madrid-Galicia 21 (1998 to 2019, expected at audit)[^S-bench-es-001].

Annex III projects (ECA; real total against initial total, computed ratios): Soto del Real-Segovia (Guadarrama) 1,380.3 to 1,702.5 m EUR (+23 %; ECA: "there were a delay and a cost overrun")[^S-bench-es-002]; Madrid-Galicia La Hiniesta-Cernadilla 211.5 to 243.4 m EUR (+15 %)[^S-bench-es-002]; Eje Atlantico Santiago-Oroso 85.5 to 101.8 m EUR (+19 %)[^S-bench-es-002]; Venta de Banos-Palencia-Leon phase I 365.8 to 384.8 m EUR (+5 %)[^S-bench-es-002]; Lleida-Martorell platform 78.1 to 73.3 m EUR (-6 %)[^S-bench-es-002]; Figueres-Perpignan 994 to 952 m EUR (-4 %; objectives not achieved)[^S-bench-es-002].

Ridership-based failures, read as failures and not as forecasts. Madrid-Valencia (press; grade D): AIReF says the line cost 4,784 m EUR, "1.204 más de los previstos", a deviation of 34 %[^S-bench-es-008]. Madrid-Leon and Eje Atlantico are among the lines below the ECA's nine-million benchmark[^S-bench-es-001].

Cross-national reference. Beria, Grimaldi, Albalate and Bel (2018) state in the abstract that "High-speed rail projects are often among the worst practices for cost overruns and demand overestimation"[^S-bench-es-010]. The abstract does not give Spanish line figures, so it is not used as a metric.

Renegotiations. No renegotiation of Spanish HSR contracts was found in the primary sources read; the TCu finding concerns extensions and deviations, and the 2019 Cortes resolution asks ADIF to "aplicar soluciones técnicas ajustadas a la demanda" (apply technical solutions adjusted to demand) and to keep long-run financial sustainability[^S-bench-es-013].

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

Summary. The evidence read is mixed to negative for intermediate and small-city stations. No study read in this pass measures a station-level ridership or land-value gain for a Spanish intermediate stop in a population-controlled design. The studies are listed by grade and direction.

1. ECA 2018, catchment analysis of audited lines (grade A; audit, not an impact study). Some stations "do not have sufficient numbers of passengers in their immediate catchment areas, and are located too close to each other"[^S-bench-es-001] (para 71). The catchment of Guadalajara-Yebes and Calatayud is "extremely small", and "there is reason to doubt the cost-efficiency and effectiveness of keeping these stations as stops on the high-speed line"[^S-bench-es-001] (para 72). Direction: negative for these two stops (the ECA's own assessment, not a measured counterfactual).
2. Albalate, Campos and Jimenez (2017), Annals of Tourism Research 65: 71-82 (grade B, abstract only). Panel of 124 municipalities, 2005 to 2012, visitors as outcome. The effects are "in general, extremely weak or just restricted to larger cities"[^S-bench-es-011]. Direction: weak or absent for small municipalities; effects restricted to larger cities (abstract wording). Not opened in full.
3. Shen, Zhao, de Abreu e Silva and Martinez (TRB 95th Annual Meeting paper; grade B, abstract only). Madrid-Seville corridor, 1990 to 2006, accessibility-based spatial autoregressive models for Madrid, Ciudad Real, Puertollano, Cordoba and Seville. The abstract says "HSR contributes to Ciudad Real with a relevant population increase" and "whereas it seldom benefits Puertollano"[^S-bench-es-020]. Direction: mixed. The same corridor's two small cities diverge. The paper's coefficients were not read.
4. Urena (2005), Revista EURE, Ciudad Real and Puertollano (grade unverified; the abstract gives objectives and no finding in the text read)[^S-bench-es-012]. No direction recorded.
5. Urena (2009), Cities 26 (p. 266), and Garmendia, Ribalaygua and Urena (2012), Cities 29 (S26-S31): citations seen in search results only, not opened (unverified). No finding recorded[^S-bench-es-015][^S-bench-es-014].
6. Naranjo Gomez, Castanho, Cabezas Fernandez and Loures (2020), Infrastructures 5(2):11, on HSR coverage of Spanish municipalities and territory without stations: MDPI returned HTTP 403 (not read; unverified)[^S-bench-es-016]. No finding recorded.
7. Betancor and Llobet (FEDEA 2015; chapter 2017), on the financial and social returns of Spanish HSL: not opened; the earlier summary is unverified and is not used for direction here[^S-bench-es-017].

Named stations, searches done 31 Oct 2026. Ciudad Real and Puertollano: covered by items 3 and 4. Guadalajara-Yebes and Calatayud: item 1 (ECA catchment verdict). Cuenca and Segovia: a web search for impact studies on these stations returned timetables and local press only (searched: web search for "estacion AVE Cuenca Segovia Guadalajara Yebes impacto estudio", 2026-10-31); no reliable impact study found. Puertollano: item 3 gives the weaker result.

Net. Mixed: Ciudad Real positive, Puertollano weak (item 3). Negative or weak for small and intermediate stops (items 1 and 2). Evidence is limited to abstracts for two of the three empirical studies, and no Spanish study read measures a station-level gain against a matched control.

Cost of intermediate stops (not an impact). Each intermediate stop adds four to 12 minutes on average, and cuts average speed by three to 16 km/h[^S-bench-es-001]. ECA's rating of stops for Guadalajara-Yebes and Calatayud is that they may not justify their cost.

Critical view (not a station study; grade C). Hoyos and Bel (2016), Cuadernos de Trabajo 71, a critical study of the proposed Basque Y, state that "las inversiones realizadas hasta la fecha no son social ni financieramente rentables"[^S-bench-es-019]. The authors' stance is critical; treat as an opinion source, not an impact estimate.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- The evidence suggests rail's share is high on 365 to 621 km corridors where HSR gives frequent, fast, door-to-door service (CNMC; ECA), and falls sharply beyond about 180 minutes even on full HSR[^S-bench-es-005][^S-bench-es-004].
- The ECA's audit tests are a usable checklist: passengers against a nine-million benchmark, catchment populations at each station, and the number of stops per line[^S-bench-es-001] (paras 37, 70-72, 79).
- Overruns on audited Spanish lines were 26 to 39 % at line level (ECA), and 43.2 % on the Madrid-Barcelona contracts against award prices (TCu via Cortes)[^S-bench-es-001][^S-bench-es-003]. These are reported outcomes, not a general rule.
- Operators in Spain ran operating losses in 2023 and 2024 on the CNMC's totals (operating ratio above 100 %), while infrastructure revenue is a separate question[^S-bench-es-005].
- Spanish costs per km are low in the ADBI account partly because the network was built on a flat, sparsely populated plateau[^S-bench-es-006]. That explanation is qualitative and is not transferable without a terrain comparison.
- Intermediate stops: the evidence read is mixed or negative for small and intermediate stations (section 8). No evidence read supports a general claim that intermediate stops gained.
