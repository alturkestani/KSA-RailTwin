# Cautionary cases: California High-Speed Rail, UK HS2, and other rail projects with published forecast or cost failures

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026 by the R1b collector. Every figure below was read on the page or document named in its footnote, except where the text says "unverified" (search snippet or page not opened) or "secondary". Cautionary cases are evidence, not a verdict on the projects: each section gives the causes as the official review or auditor wrote them, and it does not assert causes the sources do not state. Figures are nominal, in the currency and price basis of the source. Conversion to SAR 2025 is generated in `README.md` (converted values: see README.md). Metric rows are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-cautionary.csv`. Reference-class candidate observations (capex, schedule, ridership) are in `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-cautionary.csv`; ratios there are computed from printed values and no uplift is set. Escalations are at the end.

Cases. A. California High-Speed Rail (Phase 1; state business plans; federal grant action). B. UK HS2 (Phase One London–West Midlands; Phase 2 cancelled in 2023). C. Taiwan High Speed Rail (opened 2007 [^S-bench-caut-033]; financial restructuring). D. Brightline Florida (privately financed higher-speed rail; ridership and revenue against the rating-case forecast). E. Portugal (Lisbon–Madrid and Lisbon–Porto lines; the ECA 2018 review). F. Channel Tunnel (ridership and revenue forecasts against outturn). Haramain (KSA) is excluded, as the brief requires.

Quality note. Most California plan figures were read through the Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) and a Senate committee analysis, because the Authority's own plan PDFs and site returned a bot-challenge page on 31 Oct 2026. The Taiwan operator's annual reports, the Oakervee review, the Railway Journal article and the Anguera full text were not reached. Those gaps are listed in Section 10.

## 3. Ridership against forecast (forecast at decision; outturn at +5 years; ratio)

No ridership level ratio (outturn over forecast) was found in the sources read for any case. Two partial observations exist:

- **Brightline Florida, growth rates.** KBRA reports that 2025 annual ridership growth was 12.8% against a rating-case forecast of 26.3%, and ticket revenue growth was 14.1% against 37.5% [^S-bench-caut-036]. These are growth rates, not levels, so they are recorded as growth-rate observations in the overruns file, not as ridership_vs_forecast rows. KBRA's release says "Performance was constrained for most of the year by limited capacity" [^S-bench-caut-079].
- **Taiwan HSR, qualitative.** The Taipei Times reports that passenger traffic declined from November of the year before the April 2009 article, "the first decline since the company launched its service" [^S-bench-caut-034]. The article attributes the decline "because of the global financial crisis" [^S-bench-caut-083]; it gives no forecast figure, so no ratio is computed [^S-bench-caut-034].
- **Channel Tunnel, qualitative.** The Anguera abstract states that the forecasts "largely and systematically overestimated the total size and growth" of the cross-Channel markets, and that revenues were "much lower than predicted" [^S-bench-caut-043].

Ridership for California (no ridership figure in the LAO analysis; the LAO notes that the station changes have "important implications (such as related to costs, ridership, and compliance with state law)" [^S-bench-caut-084]) and HS2 (no ridership forecast in the NAO 2026 reset report; searched 2026-10-31) is a gap (Section 10).

## 4. Farebox recovery or operating ratio (definition used by the source)

No farebox recovery or operating ratio was found for any case. Partial evidence:

- **Brightline Florida.** KBRA anticipates 2025 operating expenses "approximately 5% higher than forecast" [^S-bench-caut-038], and the release says the January 2026 debt service payment "required a draw from the debt service reserve account" (DSRA) [^S-bench-caut-082]. KBRA's base case has liquidity exhausted and a "potential default by January 2027" [^S-bench-caut-060]. These are debt-service measures, not an operating ratio.
- **California.** The LAO's 2008 analysis said operating and maintenance costs were "unknown" but "probably in excess of $1 billion a year" [^S-bench-caut-007]. This is a 2008 estimate of annual cost, not a farebox ratio, and its price basis is not stated; it is recorded here, not in the metric file.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, and the 200–800 km band

No density or city-size threshold was found in these sources. The 200–800 km band is hypothesis, not a finding, and this dossier does not test it against air or car. Distance facts that the cases do give:

- Brightline Florida is described as a 235-mile service [^S-bench-caut-051]. The unit conversion gives about 378 km (235 × 1.609344). That length lies inside the 200–800 km band, but KBRA's evidence concerns ridership and revenue, not mode choice against air or car. No mode-share or air–rail substitution study was read for this case.
- The California SM–NB segment is 162 miles [^S-bench-caut-014] (about 261 km by conversion). The LAO notes the segment is shorter than the previously contemplated segment (171 miles) [^S-bench-caut-014].
- No mode-share-by-distance study was read for any case. Lessons on the band are in the benchmark dossiers for the operating countries, not here.

The LAO describes these as scope reductions and states the plan "does not mention that it assumes changes to the Merced and Bakersfield station locations" [^S-bench-caut-016]. Station spacing and timetable were not found.

- **HS2, Taiwan, Portugal, Channel Tunnel.** No reliable data found for station spacing or stopping patterns (searched: the NAO 2026 reset report, the Taipei Times article, the ECA 2018 review, Anguera 2006 abstract; 2026-10-31).

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

### A. California High-Speed Rail

- **Construction contracts, Central Valley.** The State Auditor reports that the risks the Authority took on by starting construction early "manifested in changes to its construction contracts" and that the three active construction projects' costs had "thus far" increased "by more than $600 million" [^S-bench-caut-068] [^S-bench-caut-001]. The Authority "estimates that it will need an additional $1.6 billion in contract changes" to finish them [^S-bench-caut-053]. The auditor's stated cause is that the Authority "had not acquired sufficient land for building" when it began construction [^S-bench-caut-061]. No printed baseline contract value was found in the summary, so these are not in the overruns file as capex observations.
- **Phase 1 system cost, estimate to estimate.** The Phase 1 (San Francisco to Anaheim) estimate was USD 33 bn in 2008 dollars in the 2008 business plan, as reported by a Senate committee analysis [^S-bench-caut-008]; USD 43 bn after the 2009 change to year-of-construction dollars [^S-bench-caut-009]; USD 98 bn in the draft 2012 plan [^S-bench-caut-010]; USD 77.3 bn in the 2018 plan [^S-bench-caut-002]; USD 128 bn in the 2024 estimate [^S-bench-caut-011]; USD 126 bn in the Draft 2026 plan on an optimised scope, and USD 231 bn "absent optimizations such as the scope changes" [^S-bench-caut-011] [^S-bench-caut-013] [^S-bench-caut-069]. The ratios in the overruns file are: 2008 to 2018, 2.34 (computed) [^S-bench-caut-008] [^S-bench-caut-002]; 2018 to 2024, 1.66 [^S-bench-caut-002] [^S-bench-caut-011]; 2009 to 2012, 2.28 [^S-bench-caut-009] [^S-bench-caut-010]; 2024 to the unoptimised 2026 figure, 1.81 [^S-bench-caut-011] [^S-bench-caut-013]. The price basis is not stated after 2009 in the sources read, so these ratios are not adjusted. The LAO notes that the scope change is "not explicitly highlighted" in the Draft 2026 plan [^S-bench-caut-070], and that the plan says "cost savings are from unspecified" optimisation measures [^S-bench-caut-071].
- **Schedule.** The auditor's timeline shows Central Valley construction completion "based on current contracts" of 2020 [^S-bench-caut-003], moved to a "new expected completion date" of March 2022 after projected contract changes [^S-bench-caut-004]: two years, computed from the printed years. The auditor also warned that "the Authority could miss the new deadline unless" construction progressed faster [^S-bench-caut-065], and that missing it could expose the state to "as much as $3.5 billion in federal funds" [^S-bench-caut-066].
- **Federal funding.** The LAO reports a "recent loss of $4 billion in federal funds" [^S-bench-caut-015]. Trade press reports the headline "FRA rescinds $4 billion in unspent federal funding to CHSRA", dated 18 Jul 2025 [^S-bench-caut-018]. The FRA release itself was not opened (transportation.gov returned access denied).
- **Renegotiation.** The auditor found that "the Authority has failed to implement sound contract management practices" [^S-bench-caut-067]. No renegotiation figure was read.

### B. UK HS2

- **Cost, like-for-like (2019 prices).** The DfT's 2020 estimate was "£35 billion to £45 billion (2019 prices)" [^S-bench-caut-024]. HS2 Ltd restated its May 2026 estimate for comparability "would equate to between £70.9 billion and £82.2 billion in 2019 prices" [^S-bench-caut-025]. The ratios are 2.03 (low ends) and 1.83 (high ends), computed [^S-bench-caut-024] [^S-bench-caut-025]. The NAO attributes the increase to "cost underestimation, inefficient delivery and scope changes" [^S-bench-caut-062].
- **Headline estimate (mixed price base).** The May 2026 estimate is "£87.7 billion and £102.7 billion (mixed price base)" [^S-bench-caut-019]. The mixed base means it is not the same price basis as the 2020 estimate, so it is not in the overruns file.
- **Estimate after Phase 2 cancellation.** The DfT's 2023 future-cost estimate for Phase 1 was "£45 billion to £54 billion (2019 prices)" [^S-bench-caut-027] (the 2020 estimate low and high ends are in the same 2019 prices). The ratios are 1.29 (low ends) and 1.20 (high ends), computed.
- **Schedule.** The NAO says DfT now expects to open the full railway "between around three and 13 years later than it expected in 2020" [^S-bench-caut-023]. The 2020 schedule window for the full railway was "2031" earliest and "2036" latest [^S-bench-caut-050] [^S-bench-caut-031]; the 2026 window is May 2040 to December 2043 [^S-bench-caut-054] [^S-bench-caut-021]. The printed years give about four and 12 years, not three and 13; the NAO's statement uses month-level dates (Escalations).
- **Reset and renegotiation.** HS2 Ltd estimates the reset will cost "£153 million" [^S-bench-caut-022] and the NAO reports the revised estimates have "a high level of uncertainty" [^S-bench-caut-073], with a more robust baseline due by spring 2027 (summary, PDF p.5). HS2 Ltd "has proposed running trains at 320 km/h" [^S-bench-caut-074], after the Secretary of State asked whether "running trains slower than 360 km/h" could reduce cost [^S-bench-caut-075]. The estimated savings are "between £1 billion and £2.5 billion" [^S-bench-caut-076], with "a longer-term reduction in benefits of around £1.3 billion" [^S-bench-caut-077] (summary, PDF p.5). The benefit–cost ratio is "between 1.5 and 6.4" in May 2026 [^S-bench-caut-028]; the NAO reports that had the current estimated costs been known when work began in 2020, the ratio "would have resulted in a benefit–cost ratio of between 0.3 and 0.4" [^S-bench-caut-078] (summary, PDF p.5).

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

These are what the sources report. They are not recommendations, and the project is not assessed against any of them.

1. **Construction risks and cost growth.** The California auditor reports that the Authority began construction before it had "sufficient land" [^S-bench-caut-061] and that the risks "manifested in changes to its construction contracts" [^S-bench-caut-068] [^S-bench-caut-001]. The NAO reports that HS2 costs had "doubled since 2020 due to cost underestimation, inefficient delivery and scope changes" [^S-bench-caut-062]. Both sources attribute the growth to several causes, and the NAO separates scope change from underestimation.
2. **Estimate basis.** The California estimates move between price bases (2008 dollars, year-of-construction dollars, unstated bases after 2009) and HS2's 2026 headline uses a mixed base [^S-bench-caut-009] [^S-bench-caut-019]. Comparing estimates across plans requires the price basis, which many official figures do not state.
3. **Scope reduction as the main cost lever.** Both the LAO on California and the NAO on HS2 describe scope and speed changes as part of their lower or revised figures [^S-bench-caut-069] [^S-bench-caut-070] [^S-bench-caut-074]. The NAO estimates these changes save "between £1 billion and £2.5 billion" [^S-bench-caut-076] and reduce benefits by "a longer-term reduction in benefits of around £1.3 billion" [^S-bench-caut-077] (summary, PDF p.5).
4. **Private-finance ridership risk.** KBRA's 2025 review of Brightline reports ridership and revenue growth below the rating case and a DSRA draw [^S-bench-caut-036] [^S-bench-caut-060]. KBRA describes its current forecast as "a conservative floor" [^S-bench-caut-080]. The evidence is about one operator's debt service, not about rail demand generally.
5. **Forecast accuracy in an early operating phase.** The Taiwan press report attributes the decline to the financial crisis [^S-bench-caut-083], and the Channel Tunnel abstract reports forecasts that "systematically overestimated" the market [^S-bench-caut-034] [^S-bench-caut-043]. The sources cover different causes and different periods; they are not combined.
6. **Cancellation and revival.** The ECA judged the Lisbon–Madrid line "too expensive in times of high government debt" [^S-bench-caut-041]; the Lisbon–Porto line was later scheduled for a 2023 tender [^S-bench-caut-042]. The sequence is documented, but the reasons for the revival are not in the sources read.

## Escalations

1. **Price basis (for R1c).** California's estimates from 2009 onward have no stated single price year in the sources read; they are recorded as unknown. The 2008 figure is in 2008 dollars and the HS2 2019-price series is explicit. Ratios across the 2009 boundary are not like-for-like. Decision needed on whether R1c uses only the explicit-basis pairs.
2. **Scope equivalence (California 2024 to 2026).** The LAO describes USD 231 bn as Phase I "absent optimizations such as the scope changes" [^S-bench-caut-013]. The overruns file treats it as the same-scope comparator to the 2024 USD 128 bn estimate, but the LAO does not say so in those words. Confirm against the 2026 plan.
3. **HS2 schedule statement versus printed years.** The NAO says the full railway is "around three" and "13 years later" than the 2020 schedule [^S-bench-caut-023]. The printed windows (2020: 2031 to 2036; 2026: 2040 to 2043) give about 4 and 12 years. The metric file keeps the NAO's statement as printed and the printed years in the overruns file.
4. **Taiwan opening month.** The Taipei Times gives February 2007 [^S-bench-caut-033]. A search summary of another source gives January 2007 (unverified). The file uses February; check against THSRC's records.
5. **Brightline ridership.** KBRA gives growth rates, not levels. The overruns file records them under kind "ridership" with notes. R1c should decide whether growth-rate ratios are admissible. Ticket revenue is also recorded under kind "ridership" because the schema has no revenue kind.
6. **Portugal ECA row.** The Annex III row is a conventional-line stretch (Évora–Caia and a technical station), not a high-speed line. Its "cost per km" (6.3 EUR m) [^S-bench-caut-040] must not be read as a high-speed cost. The ECA cost tables do not include Portugal in the overrun analysis.
7. **Unverified press figures not used.** The Taiwan restructuring figures, the Channel Tunnel press figures, the Portugal 2012 cancellation amounts, the Brightline Fitch and S&P actions, the press reading of the unoptimised California figure in "today's dollars" (search summary), and the FRA "not one high-speed track" statement are recorded as unverified or not used. None feeds a metric row.
8. **Overlap with other dossiers.** ECA Table 5 overrun data for France, Spain, Germany and Italy lines is in those country dossiers and is not repeated here, to avoid double counting in R1c.
9. **Rounding in computed rows.** Ratios and percentages are computed from printed values and rounded to three decimals (ratio) or one decimal (percent). The conversion of miles to km uses 1.609344 km per mile.
