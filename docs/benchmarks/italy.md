# Italy: Alta Velocità / Alta Capacità (RFI infrastructure; FS group, Trenitalia Frecciarossa; Italo (NTV) open access)

Scope and method. Research on 31 Oct 2026. Figures are in the currency and price basis of each source and are quoted as printed; no currency or price conversion is done in this file. Converted values are generated in `docs/benchmarks/README.md` (`rail.benchmarks summarise`, A-cost-002 with `cities/ksa/costs/index.yaml`, CP2 P2a; formerly A-bench-001). Every figure below was read in this session on the page or PDF named in its footnote, except where marked "secondary", "not opened" or "unverified". The backbone is the European Court of Auditors Special Report 19/2018 (grade A), which covers the two Italian lines the audit assessed and the Italian annex tables. The rest is peer-reviewed or operator research (grades B and C), plus one press report of Italo's 2017 accounts (grade C). No Italo or RFI primary filing was reached. Metric rows: `cities/ksa/benchmarks/metrics-italy.csv`. Overrun candidates: `cities/ksa/benchmarks/overruns-italy.csv`. Escalations are at the end.

Range across sources. ## 3. Ridership vs forecast (forecast at decision, outcome at +5 years; ratio)

No reliable data found for a line-level forecast-at-decision versus outturn ratio (searched: ECA SR 19/2018; Beria et al. 2018; FS Research Centre slides 2023 and 2025; the WCTR paper; 31 Oct 2026). Metric row not created.

What the sources say:
- Beria et al. (2018) state that the Italian programme "shows excellent demand performances" [^S-bench-it-031], and that the early figures were "not particularly impressive, with actual patronage well below the expectations", citing Beria and Grimaldi (2011), which was not opened [^S-bench-it-032].
- Unofficial 2013 estimates for Trenitalia plus NTV by line section: Torino-Milano 4 m, Milano-Bologna 13 m, Bologna-Firenze 18 m, Firenze-Roma 17.5 m, Roma-Napoli 7.5 m passengers (metric rows M-it-034 to M-it-038). The 2010 Trenitalia-only comparators are in the same table [^S-bench-it-032]. These are not counts and not forecasts.
- Italo declared 6.6 m passengers (2014) and 9.1 m (2015) [^S-bench-it-033]; press reports 12.8 m for 2017 [^S-bench-it-027]. The 2015 to 2017 growth is computed at +41 % from these two printed values. No forecast was found to compare against.
- Milan-Rome: the FS analysis reports that the estimated demand on the relation rose by 30 % between 2008 and 2019 [^S-bench-it-021]. The slide 6 ratio for the same comparison does not reconcile with that text (Escalations).

Beria et al. (2018) also state in general terms that HSR projects are "often among the worst practices for cost overruns and demand overestimation" [^S-bench-it-031]. That is a literature statement, not an Italian outturn, and is not used as a ratio.

## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, and the 200–800 km band (air and car)

Density and city-size thresholds: no reliable data found. The sources read (ECA, Beria et al., FS slides, ART deck) give no density or city-size threshold for Italian HSR. No dedicated literature search on thresholds was run.

The 200–800 km band (hypothesis; tested, not asserted)

(a) Inside the band, one corridor. Milan-Rome is 471 km straight-line [^S-bench-it-022]. Rail share of the trips on the relation rose from 36 % (2008) to 74 % (2019), and the aviation chart labels show 49 % (2008) and 17 % (2019) [^S-bench-it-019][^S-bench-it-020]. The source is an operator analysis of survey-based shares (grade C); the survey method is not described in the slides. The FS slides print the Milan-Rome rail distance as 571 or 574 km in a table without a text label [^S-bench-it-022], and a 2010 conference paper describes a direct service "covering 720 Km's in 3 hours" [^S-bench-it-025]. The distance conflict is in Escalations.

(b) Lower end of the band (about 200–250 km). Rome-Naples (204 km) and Florence-Rome (254 km) are cited with travel times in a 2010 conference paper [^S-bench-it-026]. No mode-share study for these pairs was found. Beria et al. (2018) state that the end segments, Milan-Turin and Rome-Naples, "are the less crowded" [^S-bench-it-039], and the Turin-Milan length in their Table 11 is 125 km [^S-bench-it-037]. This is evidence that demand is not monotonic in distance on the Italian network, which is a mixed signal for any single band.

(c) Car side. Borsati and Albalate (peer-reviewed postprint, 2019 working paper) test motorway traffic in tolled sectors, 2001 to 2017. They find that "neither HSR openings nor the opening of on-track competition led to a modal shift from motorway to HSR services", and that HSR expansion "had a slightly positive impact on motorway traffic" [^S-bench-it-024]. This is negative for the assumption that HSR in Italy took car trips in that period; it is one study of motorway-adjacent sectors.

(d) Air side beyond Milan-Rome. A Cascetta and Coppola (ETC 2011) paper, cited in a search snippet as framing HSR against air on a route-length range, was not opened (repository returned 403). No figure from it is used.

Reading. Italy provides one corridor inside the band with a large rail share gain (a) and one car-side test that is negative for substitution (c). The lower end is mixed (b). The Italian evidence neither supports nor rules out a 200–800 km band, and it does not test its edges. This is a hypothesis for KSA to test with its own demand data, not a finding.

## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

- Stopping pattern. The 2010 conference paper describes a direct Rome-Milan service, 3 hours, route length printed as 720 km [^S-bench-it-025] (metric M-it-039; year not printed). Current stopping patterns were not verified: the Trenitalia timetable pages returned search snippets only. No reliable data found for the 2026 stop list for Milan-Rome, Milan-Bologna or Bologna-Florence.
- Intermediate station. Reggio Emilia AV Mediopadana opened in 2013 as the only intermediate stop on Milan-Bologna [^S-bench-it-016]. It sits in an agricultural area near the A1 motorway, about 4 km from the city centre (FS 2025, slide 5) [^S-bench-it-016].
- Frequency. 34 daily connections each direction for the new entrant on Turin-Salerno (2017-18) [^S-bench-it-013].
- Saturation. ART (17 Sep 2025) says the AV Roma-Firenze line is restricted to trains above 200 km/h. It notes that since the early 2000s an exemption has let regional trains at 150 km/h onto the line, and it refers to "saturazione sulla rete AV" (saturation of the AV network) [^S-bench-it-041].
- Station spacing (km between stations): no reliable data found.

## 7. Overruns (cost, schedule) and renegotiations

Cost, line level (ECA). The ECA could not compute line-level overruns for Turin-Salerno or Milan-Venice because no initial line estimate was available (ECA Table 5 note) [^S-bench-it-004]. The audit's aggregate statement that all audited high-speed lines had overruns above 25 % is not attributed to the Italian lines in the table [^S-bench-it-004]. No Italian line-level overrun percentage is therefore recorded.

Cost, project level (ECA Annex III). Four Italian contract rows give initial versus real total cost: Treviglio-Brescia first lot 644 to 644.2 m EUR (+0.03 %, computed); civil works phase 26.4 to 26.4 m EUR (0 %); Tratta Campana Roma-Napoli 273 to 273 m EUR (0 %); Torino-Salerno Activity 6 712.7 to 713 m EUR (+0.04 %, computed) [^S-bench-it-008][^S-bench-it-009]. These are small sub-contracts and cannot be read as line totals. The column order was checked against the PDF (Escalations). A fifth row, Brenner southern access, is excluded because its figures are asterisked and show a scope reduction [^S-bench-it-010].

Cost, programme level (Beria et al. 2018). The Turin-Salerno total is given as 10.7 bn EUR in the initial plan and 32.0 bn EUR in 2006 [^S-bench-it-034]. The ratio computed from these two printed values is 2.99 (candidate OV-it-005), but it mixes price years: footnote 8 of the paper equates 10.7 bn EUR with 15.5 bn EUR in 2006 money [^S-bench-it-034], and the like-for-like comparison of 15.5 to 32.0 bn is the "doubled in real terms" statement, a ratio of about 2.06 computed from those figures. The authors say the increase is mostly due to design choices, and cite the 1997 redefinition of the line "when the Parliament requested a total redefinition of line characteristics" [^S-bench-it-038]. They argue the problem lies in "deliberate choices of overinvestment, overdesign and overquality" [^S-bench-it-031]. This is the authors' interpretation; the RFI (2007) primary was not opened.

Design-choice evidence (ECA). ECA reports that the Venice-Trieste design review found a 300 km/h new line costs 7.5 bn EUR against 1.8 bn EUR for an upgraded 200 km/h line, for 10 minutes of travel time [^S-bench-it-007]. ECA says that this kind of design-speed review is practised in Italy and Germany and not in the other visited states [^S-bench-it-007].

Schedule, line level. ECA Table 2: Turin-Salerno planning 1987, works 1994, in operation 2009; Milan-Venice planning 1995, works 2003, expected 2028 (asterisk) [^S-bench-it-002]. ECA paragraph 54 says the Milan-Venice delay is forecast at 18 years against initial estimates, the longest among the audited lines [^S-bench-it-015]. Metric M-it-027.

Schedule, project level. The Tratta Campana project was on time but had a 3-year delay in the opening of the line [^S-bench-it-009] (M-it-028).

Renegotiations: no reliable data found (searched: ECA; Beria et al.; 31 Oct 2026).

Failures reported by the sources, stated without advocacy: the Milan-Venice delay (forecast 18 years); the Turin-Salerno cost escalation per the 2018 paper (price basis disputed); the 1997 redesign [^S-bench-it-038]. Not failures per the sources: the open-access fare effects on Turin-Salerno and Milan-Venice (S-bench-it-042, -014), which the sources report as decreasing fares, though the effect faded [^S-bench-it-043].

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain?

Italy has one intermediate station with an empirical study found: Reggio Emilia AV Mediopadana, opened 2013 as the only intermediate stop on Milan-Bologna [^S-bench-it-016]. No ridership or travel-time study of Mediopadana was found (no reliable data found; press pages only). No empirical study was found for Afragola (Naples) and no actual ridership was found; press pre-opening projections were not used.

| study | method | sample | finding (as the source reports it) | direction | grade | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bottasso, Conti, Fiduccia, Robbiano and Tartaglia (2025), Springer chapter | difference-in-differences on Italy's OMI housing market data | Mediopadana (quasi-random location between Bologna and Milan), residential and commercial property | residential prices up 2 to 6 % in areas 20-30 km or 15-30 min from the station; commercial property minimal or no increase | positive for residential; null for commercial | B (book chapter; full text paywalled; abstract read) | [^S-bench-it-030] |
| FS Research Centre, Cerullo et al. (2025), conference slides | spatial-temporal urban development index (sparse PCA) on a hexagonal grid; DiD across isochrones (15-min walk, 15-min bike, 30-min bike) | Mediopadana (and Rho Fiera, a metropolitan site) | DiD +4.5 % within 15-min walk and cycle; +1.2 % at 30-min cycle; the area was already growing before the station | positive but small; pre-existing trend | C (operator research, slides, not peer reviewed) | [^S-bench-it-017][^S-bench-it-018] |

Mixed and negative elements, stated plainly:
- The two studies measure different outcomes (house prices, an index of urban fabric). They do not measure ridership.
- The residential-price effect is positive and the commercial effect is null in the chapter [^S-bench-it-030]. The FS slides say the area "was already experiencing growth before the station was built" and that the station "reinforced this process" [^S-bench-it-018]. The pre-trend point means the effect cannot be read as the station's own growth.
- No study found tests whether an intermediate station gained riders relative to a non-station baseline. Small-town ridership evidence for Italy: no reliable data found.

## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)

- Core-corridor demand and construction cost were both high in the Italian record. The Italian evidence reports strong demand on the core corridor [^S-bench-it-031][^S-bench-it-032] and among the highest construction cost per km in the ECA sample [^S-bench-it-006][^S-bench-it-044]. The source authors attribute much of the cost to design choices [^S-bench-it-031][^S-bench-it-038], which is an interpretation, not a settled finding.
- Open access. Competition on Turin-Milan-Venice cut the incumbent's fares in the short to medium term (about 21 to 26 % on Milano-Venezia within 84 to 140 days of entry) and the reduction did not stay stable in the long term [^S-bench-it-042][^S-bench-it-043]. ECA reports at least 24 % lower fares and 34 daily connections each way on Turin-Salerno [^S-bench-it-014][^S-bench-it-013].
- Capacity. ART reports restrictions on the AV Roma-Firenze line and saturation of the AV network in 2025 [^S-bench-it-041].
- Small towns. One intermediate station shows a positive residential-price effect with pre-existing growth, and no ridership evidence [^S-bench-it-030][^S-bench-it-018]. The evidence does not show that intermediate stations in small towns gained riders.
- Distance band. Italian evidence covers one corridor with a large rail share gain [^S-bench-it-019]; the lower-distance evidence is mixed [^S-bench-it-039][^S-bench-it-037]; the car-side evidence is negative for substitution [^S-bench-it-024]. This is not enough to derive a band.
- Any transfer to KSA needs KSA demand, cost and price data. The per-km figures here are nominal, from 2006 to 2018 and "current terms", and are not converted in this file.
