# Distributional analysis (W4/W7, R5)

**Synthetic demand, uncalibrated: scenario comparison, not a forecast.** Method: `docs/social.md` Part B (DfT TAG A4.2, S-soc-051: each group's share of user benefits against its share of the population). Code: `economics/distribution.py`; tables generated by `uv run python -m economics.cli distribution` from `runs/cba/<case>/` and `runs/cba/inputs/un_wpp_medium-2050/zones.parquet`.

**Who gains.** User benefits (rule-of-a-half generalised-cost savings valued at the purpose VoT) are allocated half to the origin and half to the destination zone of each OD (docs/social.md B1 step 3a), summed by region, and compared with each region's population share. Regional shares come from the medium population scenario at 2050 (A-cba-310); the SAR ranges apply them to the Monte Carlo PV of user benefits (medium scenario, 60 years, Green Book 2026). Foreign pilgrims and tourists are not in these tables (no generalised-cost model for them; docs/social.md B4 asks for a separate row, which is empty by construction).

**Income bands.** No income-by-zone source exists (docs/social.md B4: HIES 2023 tables not available). The proxy named there is used and labelled: regions grouped into terciles of the Saudi unemployment rate, GASTAT LFS Q4 2025 (S-soc-015, S-soc-016; values in `cities/ksa/assumptions/cba_programme.yaml`, A-cba-311). It is a regional labour-market proxy, not household income.

**Who pays.** The fiscal cost to the state (PV of capex, opex and renewals less fares, fully public delivery, A-cba-312; W6/R7 assess delivery models) and the fares paid by users (PV). Tax incidence is not modelled (docs/social.md B4). Fare-to-income ratios are not available.

**Who is left out.** Towns of 10,000+ (register `cities/ksa/candidates.parquet`) in the regions a case serves that are more than 30 km (A-cba-313) from every station of the case's line set, and freight-only route-km (none: every candidate carries passengers).
