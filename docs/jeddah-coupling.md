# Jeddah decongestion coupling with TrafficTwin (R4 task 4)

**Label: synthetic demand, uncalibrated: scenario comparison, not a forecast.** Every TrafficTwin number is a 10 % population sample with capacity scaling, seed 1, on a gridlock-affected baseline. The teleport share is the quality flag. Read deltas, not levels.

## 1. What TrafficTwin can and cannot represent

TrafficTwin (the read-only checkout) simulates the internal trips of a synthetic Jeddah resident population, about 370,000 persons in the 10 % sample. It has no external or through demand: its gateways are network topology, not demand (TrafficTwin `docs/fidelity.md`, `docs/network-qa.md`).

The R4 rail scenarios change two kinds of trips:
- **Intercity trips.** Car trips to and from Jeddah move to rail (Haramain, the Landbridge, the S2 trunks).
- **Commuting from the belt.** S4 commuter-belt towns commute into Jeddah by rail.

Both are external to TrafficTwin's demand. The only rail effect TrafficTwin can carry is the car shift between rail-station catchments **inside** the city. TrafficTwin has no rail transform, so that shift is written as its `pt_line` transform (TrafficTwin `docs/policies.md`):
- the stops are the in-city stations;
- the headway is the rail headway;
- `shift_share` comes from the R4 mode-choice result.

The line's vehicles are buses at the rail headway. That is a conservative road-load proxy, because a train adds no road vehicles (A-r4-cpl-003).

| family | stations inside the TrafficTwin network | runnable | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haramain in-city segment (S0; S1 adds the co-located Landbridge Jeddah terminal, A-r4-acc-004) | HHR Al-Sulaymaniyah, HHR Airport (KAIA) | yes | two in-city stations |
| S4 Jeddah commuter belts (000°, 045°, 315°) | the Jeddah terminal only | no | the belt stations (Khulays, Al-Kamil, Al-Jumum, Thuwal) and their commuters are outside TrafficTwin's population |
| S2 Riyadh–Jeddah trunk, S2 Jeddah–Yanbu | the Jeddah terminal proxy only | no | the shifted intercity car trips are external to TrafficTwin's demand |

Measuring the belt and trunk effects needs an external-demand layer in TrafficTwin (inbound commuter and intercity car trips at the gateways). This project cannot add one, because TrafficTwin is read-only here. It is listed as an open item.

## 2. Policy YAML (validated with TrafficTwin's own `sim.schemas.Policy`)

`coupling/policies/r4_hhr_jeddah_kaia.yaml` is generated by `uv run python -m coupling.jeddah policies --seed 1` from `cities/ksa/assumptions/access_coupling.yaml`:
- `pt_line` HHRJ, both directions, stops at the two HHR stations' access roads (A-r4-cpl-005: TrafficTwin snaps a stop to an aligned bus edge within 200 m; the station points are 266–380 m from one);
- `headway_s` 3600 (the HHR headway, S-r1a-011); hours 05:00–23:00 (A-r4-cpl-004); catchment 1,500 m (A-r4-cpl-002);
- `shift_share` **0.143** (A-r4-cpl-001): the rail share of the Jeddah ↔ Jeddah zone pairs whose rail path uses Al-Sulaymaniyah and KAIA in run `runs/r4/demand/S0-r4-2025-base-s1` (5.67 M trips a year on those pairs, 0.81 M by rail in the model).

The test `tests/test_coupling_jeddah.py` validates the YAML against TrafficTwin's pydantic model, importing it read-only.

```

  --policy <repo>/coupling/policies/r4_hhr_jeddah_kaia.yaml --daytype weekday --sample 0.10 --seed 1 \

```
(`uv run python -m coupling.jeddah command --policy r4_hhr_jeddah_kaia` prints it with the absolute paths.)

Run setup:
- **Outputs:** go to `runs/coupling/` in this repo.
- **Baselines:** TrafficTwin's two weekday seed-1 baselines were cloned there with APFS `cp -c`, so they used no new disk.
- **No writes in TrafficTwin:** no file under the checkout is newer than the run, `git status` shows only a pre-existing untracked folder, and no bytecode was written.

Run facts:
- **Disk:** 47 GiB free before; the run wrote 176 MB.
- **Wall time:** about 7 min.
- **Run id:** `r4_hhr_jeddah_kaia` (`runs/coupling/r4_hhr_jeddah_kaia/`).
- **Comparison baseline:** `baseline-weekday-f0.1-s1-ae764558-346893f2-a998e1` (TrafficTwin network v2, population hash 346893f2).
- **Matched control:** none needed. The runner adds one only when baseline vehicles are re-routed, and here all 389,296 vehicle trips kept their baseline route (0 routed anew).

## 4. Result (seed 1)

What the policy changed: 18 car tours lie within the two catchments, 4 of them shift (5 vehicles before, 0 after), and 36 bus departures are added.

| KPI | baseline | policy | delta | delta % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vehicle-hours (VHT) | 1,940,325 h | 1,941,245 h | +920 h | +0.1 % |
| mean delay per trip | 1,172.6 s | 1,174.2 s | +1.6 s | +0.1 % |
| AM-peak (06–09) mean delay | 1,296.8 s | 1,285.6 s | −11.2 s | −0.9 % |
| vehicle-km | 55,719,024 | 55,748,313 | +29,289 | +0.1 % |
| teleports per loaded vehicle (quality flag) | 0.2826 | 0.2823 | −0.0003 | −0.1 % |

**Reading.** In the model, the HHR in-city shift changes Jeddah's road KPIs by about ±0.1 % (AM-peak delay −0.9 %).
- The bus proxy's own road load (36 departures × the capacity scale) is larger than the 4 tours it removes, so the sign of VHT is the proxy's artefact.
- With 28 % of vehicles teleporting, these deltas are within noise.
- TrafficTwin's own placebo test shows routing alone can move city delay by 20 % (`docs/policies.md` there).

What the result does show is that the in-city part of the rail effect is negligible at TrafficTwin's scale. It does not estimate the intercity or belt decongestion, which TrafficTwin cannot represent (§1).

## 5. Seeds 2–3 and the seed band: not run

- TrafficTwin's runner looks the baseline up by seed. Seed 1 is the only weekday seed with a cached population.
- For seeds 2 and 3, `sim.population.synthesize.load_or_synthesize` would build a new population and write its cache inside the read-only checkout. The edge cache under `data/policies/` exists for both networks, so the population cache is the blocker.
- Estimate had the rule allowed it: 2 × (baseline about 28 min, TrafficTwin `docs/obhur-bridge.md` + policy about 7 min) ≈ 70 min of wall time and about 2 × (0.35 + 0.18) ≈ 1.1 GB.
- **Status: pending.** This is not a disk problem. Running the extra seeds needs the population caches for seeds 2–3 built inside TrafficTwin (by Tariq, or with his approval) or a TrafficTwin option to place the cache elsewhere.
