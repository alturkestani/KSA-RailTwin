## 3. Failures (exact evidence)

**F1 — inferred legal position on fare-setting authority (criterion 5).**
- `cities/ksa/assumptions/funding_r7.yaml:153` (`allocation.fully_public_sar.revenue`): mechanism "state company sets fares within the TGA usage-fee policy and keeps them", why "Railway Law Art. 18: TGA sets the usage-fee policy", precedents `[S-fund-016]`.
- `:161` (`public_with_private_om.revenue`): why "fare-setting stays with the owner under the TGA usage-fee policy".
- `:177` (`ppp_dbfom_demand_risk.revenue`): mechanism "concessionaire sets fares within a regulated cap", why "TGA's usage-fee policy bounds fare-setting (Railway Law Art. 18)".
- What the source says: S-fund-016 (`docs/sources/r1d-fund.csv`) quotes Art. 18 "The Authority shall set a policy for determining and collecting usage fees." The Railway Law M/159 (MISA/Bureau of Experts translation, fetched 4 Nov 2026) Art. 1 defines **"Usage Fee: A fee charged pursuant to a use agreement"**, and "Use Agreement: A network use agreement or station use agreement" between an infrastructure manager (or operator) and another licensee; Art. 18(2) says the infrastructure manager or operator "shall determine and collect the usage fees and use such fees to finance his activities". Art. 18 therefore governs access charges between licensees. `docs/funding.md:342` (R1d, verified) itself reads it as the "usage-fee (access-charge) policy". No cited source states who sets or approves **passenger fares**.
- Propagation: 17 of the 30 generated matrices contain "usage-fee policy" in the revenue row; `docs/delivery-models.md:75, 132, 188, 242, 296` (revenue rows of the recommended-model matrices); `recommendations.yaml` has no `fare_setting_authority` legal item.
- Required: cite Art. 18 for access charges only; add a legal item `fare_setting_authority` = **unclear** with the question (who sets or approves passenger fares on a new line: TGA under the Railway Law or its Regulations, the operator, or the concession/O&M contract?); reword the three revenue mechanisms to "state (or concessionaire) keeps fares; fare-setting authority unclear"; regenerate the matrices and `docs/delivery-models.md`.

**F2 — corridor net-fiscal-cost series are internally inconsistent across horizons (check 10).**
- Cause: `funding/fiscal.py:371-380` takes the 60-year value of public-kind models from the judge (`fiscal_cost_p50`; `pvc_pX` + scaled residual, A-fund-107) when the row exists, and the 30/40-year values from the cash-flow engine (`flows`); corridor scores hold only the 60-year row, so the two methods are mixed inside one series. They are not the same quantity (the judge's quantiles are joint over all draws; the engine varies capex only).
- Evidence (`docs/fiscal-exposure.md`, fully public rows, 30 / 40 / 60 y): `:97` Al-Kharj spur P10 **4,478 / 4,803 / 3,945**, P90 **20,445 / 20,771 / 19,508** (the engine's own 60-year values would be 5,258 / 7,523 / 21,226); `:109` Riyadh belt P90 22,402 / 23,299 / **22,824**; `:132` Dhahran P90 1,824 / 1,854 / **1,757**; `:143` Duruma P90 1,079 / 1,111 / **1,097**. For corridors whose fares cover 41 % (spur), 2 % (belt) or less than nothing (Dhahran, Duruma) of opex, the 60-year cost cannot be below the 40-year cost. The same values feed `recommendations.yaml` (corridor `options.*.net_fiscal_cost_pv`) and `docs/delivery-models.md:17-20`. The programme tables (`:52`, `:70`, `:35`) are monotone because the layout scores hold rows at every horizon.
- Consequence: no recommendation changes (model rankings within a horizon use one method), but the published corridor ranges are contradictory on their face. Required: one method per corridor series (engine for all three horizons, or judge only where the row exists), stated per cell; add a test that the net fiscal cost is non-decreasing in the horizon when PV fares < PV opex.
