# Benchmark dossier

Rule 2: every country the brief names appears, with findings or "no reliable data found". Rule 1: every figure cited with `S-xxx`.

```

## 4. Farebox recovery / operating ratio (definition used by the source)
## 5. Thresholds: population density, city sizes, the distance band where rail wins vs air and car (test the 200–800 km band; cite the studies)
## 6. Station spacing, timetable and stopping patterns

## 8. Small-town evidence: did intermediate stations gain? (studies, direction of effect, grade; mixed/negative stated plainly)
## 9. Lessons for KSA (evidence-based, non-advocacy)
## 10. Gaps: "no reliable data found" items
## Sources
```

## Metrics (W5) and how to normalise
Cost/km: state the price year and currency of the source; convert with the register's index (`cost-register`); keep the nominal figure too. Ridership: annual passengers and passenger-km with the year. Farebox: define (operating revenue / operating cost, or incl. renewals) as the source does. Distances and travel times: between city pairs, with the mode shares if published.

## Evidence grading (every figure carries one)
`A` primary official (operator, ministry, statistics office, audit court); `B` peer-reviewed or audited study; `C` reputable secondary (ITF/OECD, UIC, World Bank, major press with named sources); `D` unverified (Wikipedia, blogs, press without source). D figures may be listed only as "to verify" and never reach the report, the cost register or the film.

## Rules that bite
- The "gare des betteraves" critique and the Shinkansen and AVE intermediate-station studies are summarised with their findings as published, including negative or mixed results.
- Cautionary cases are evidence, not rhetoric: same template, same grades.
- No country is dropped; a thin dossier with explicit gaps is valid, a missing dossier is a FAIL.
- Each dossier ends with a `## Sources` list; every `S-xxx` is in `docs/sources/index.csv`.
